Unit1

Squeeze and rebuild

Chapter 1 · C01 to C02

Source factU03-C01 The running toy: eight points near the line y = 2x, with t from -3.5 to 3.5 in steps of 1 and fixed noise from seed 8 (standard deviation 0.2). The bottleneck is one-dimensional. The encoder projects onto w = (1, 2). The decoder multiplies back by w.

Source factU03-C01 The eight latent codes are (-3.639, -2.607, -1.609, -0.528, 0.315, 1.485, 2.423, 3.571). The mean squared reconstruction error is 0.0139. A baseline that always predicts the data mean has MSE 27.3794. The bottleneck keeps the line structure and drops only the noise.

Source factU03-C02 Representation use: the codes are features that preserve the ordering of the points along the line. Generative use: fit a Gaussian to the codes (mean near 0, variance 5.4745), draw a fresh z, decode to z w. Sample z = 1.2: (1.2, 2.4). Sample z = -2.0: (-2.0, -4.0). New points on the line, never in the dataset.

8→1eight 2-D points, one number
Nugget 01 · U03-C01
The lesson

What happened. Eight 2-D points passed through one number. Error 0.0139 against a baseline of 27.3794.

Why it matters. An autoencoder is a learned projection. The optimal linear 1-D bottleneck is the top principal component. The residual is the noise.

Principle. A bottleneck wider than the data dimension learns the identity: perfect reconstruction, meaningless codes. Shrink the bottleneck until the error moves.

UNIT 01
2 jobsfeatures and generation, one machine
Nugget 02 · U03-C02
The lesson

What happened. The same encoder and decoder did two jobs. As features, the codes preserved order. As a generator, a fitted Gaussian plus the decoder made new points.

Why it matters. The deterministic autoencoder generates only with an extra step: a prior over z. Without that step, decoding a random z is undefined behavior.

Principle. Representation keeps information about x. Generation defines a law over x. Name the job the machine does.

Unit2

Two jobs, one machine

Chapter 2 · C03 to C06

Source factU03-C03 The VAE makes the encoder stochastic: q(z|x) = N(mu(x), sigma(x)^2). It fixes the prior p(z) = N(0, 1). It trains with the ELBO: E_q[log p(x|z)] - KL(q(z|x)||p(z)). For x = (1, 2) with mu = 0.9, sigma = 0.4, decoder N(z w, 0.5^2 I): reconstruction -2.1516 nats, KL 0.9013 nats, ELBO -3.0529 nats.

The KL term regularizes the codes toward the prior, so the prior becomes a valid sampler at test time.
U03-C03, on why the VAE can sample

Source factU03-C04 The Gaussian KL has a closed form: KL = -0.5 × (1 + log sigma^2 - mu^2 - sigma^2). At mu = 0.9, sigma = 0.4: 0.9013 nats. No samples. One formula. The variance term is minimized at sigma = 1, the mean term at mu = 0.

Source factU03-C05 Give the toy VAE a second latent dimension. Dimension 1 learns mu = 0.9, sigma = 0.4, KL = 0.9013. Dimension 2 learns mu = 0, sigma = 1, KL = 0.0000. The decoder ignores dimension 2 entirely. That is posterior collapse: q(z_2|x) = p(z_2) for all x. It transmits zero information about x.

Source factU03-C06 The beta-VAE objective: E_q[log p(x|z)] - beta × KL(q||p). On the toy: beta = 0.1 gives -2.2417. Beta = 1.0 gives -3.0529, the plain VAE. Beta = 4.0 gives -5.7567. Raising beta punishes latent information harder. Rate falls. Distortion rises.

Illustration Ride the beta knob. This toy uses the lesson constants (reconstruction -2.1516, KL 0.9013) and recomputes the objective for any beta.

beta: 1.00

0.90/0.00nats per dimension, one died
Nugget 03 · U03-C05
The lesson

What happened. Dimension 1 carried 0.9013 nats. Dimension 2 carried 0.0000. The ELBO looked fine either way.

Why it matters. The KL gradient pushes (mu, sigma) toward (0, 1). On a dimension the decoder does not need, only the KL gradient acts. It wins. A fully collapsed VAE is a decoder-only density model wearing a VAE costume.

Principle. Diagnose with per-dimension KL on a validation batch. Fix with KL annealing first: it is the cheapest. Never fix it by deleting the KL term: the KL is load-bearing.

UNIT 02
β 0.1·1·4objective fell by construction
Nugget 04 · U03-C06
The lesson

What happened. Beta 0.1, 1.0, 4.0 gave -2.2417, -3.0529, -5.7567. The objective fell as beta rose, by construction.

Why it matters. Beta is a Lagrange multiplier on the rate. Beta above 1 buys disentangling at the cost of blur. Beta below 1 buys fidelity at the cost of a meaningless prior. At beta 4 the number says nothing about the likelihood.

Principle. Tune beta on held-out likelihood or the downstream task, never on the weighted sum.

Unit3

The price of a prior

Chapter 3 · C07 to C09

Source factU03-C07 Four codebook vectors in R^2: e1 = (0,0), e2 = (2,4), e3 = (-2,-4), e4 = (0,3). The datapoint x = (1,2) picks one. Squared distances: 5.0, 5.0, 45.0, 2.0. A soft posterior with logits -d^2/2: (0.1543, 0.1543, 0.0000, 0.6914). Code 4 wins. The posterior entropy is 0.8318 nats against a uniform prior entropy of log 4 = 1.3863. Information used: 0.5545 nats.

Source factU03-C09 Let z ~ N(mu, 1) and y = 1 if z > 0 else 0. The expected output is Phi(mu). At mu = 0, the true gradient is phi(0) = 0.3989. The straight-through estimator copies the upstream gradient through the hard step as if the step were the identity: estimated gradient 1.0. Bias: 0.6011. At mu = 2.0, the true gradient is 0.0540 and the bias grows to 0.9460.

1 of 4only one code ever won
Nugget 05 · U03-C07
The lesson

What happened. Four codes, one winner at 0.6914, one effectively impossible at 0.0000. The latent was a choice, not a location.

Why it matters. The ELBO keeps its shape with a categorical KL. The gradient does not survive: a hard choice is not differentiable. Codebook collapse is the discrete twin of posterior collapse: only a few codes ever win.

Principle. Diagnose with code usage counts on a validation batch. Restart dead codes at random encoder outputs.

UNIT 03
0.3989/1.0true slope vs copied slope
Nugget 06 · U03-C09
The lesson

What happened. True slope 0.3989. Copied slope 1.0. The bias does not shrink with more samples.

Why it matters. Straight-through pretends the step is the identity on the backward pass. It is biased by construction. It works in practice because the bias points in a useful direction often enough, and the alternative carries high variance.

Principle. Never trust the straight-through gradient magnitude. It is not a gradient of anything. Tune the learning rate on the training curve, not on gradient theory.

Unit4

Codes as choices

Chapter 4 · C10 to C12

Source factU03-C10 The likelihood is the decoder probabilistic contract. Gaussian on x = (1,2) with reconstruction (0.9, 1.9) and c = 1: NLL 1.8479 nats. Bernoulli on x = 1 with prediction 0.8: NLL 0.2231 nats. The blur demo: a target that is 0 or 10 with equal probability. The MSE-optimal constant predictor is 5, with MSE 25. It never outputs a real data value.

Source factU03-C11 The Gaussian rate-distortion function for source variance 4: R(D) = 0.5 × log(4/D) nats. D = 0.25 needs 1.3863 nats. D = 1.0 needs 0.6931 nats. D = 2.0 needs 0.3466 nats. The toy VAE sits at rate 0.9013, distortion 2.1516 nats. The frontier at D = 2.1516 needs only 0.3099 nats. The toy overpays: the price of the diagonal Gaussian family and the fixed decoder.

Source factU03-C12 Bits per dimension: BPD = -log2 p(x) / D. For the toy VAE, the ELBO bounds log p(x) at -3.0529 nats, so BPD ≤ 2.2022 bits per dimension. It is the compression view: how many bits an ideal coder would need per dimension under this model. It hides sample quality: a model can have great BPD and blurry samples, because likelihood rewards covering mass, not looking sharp.

5two modes blurred into their mean
Nugget 07 · U03-C10
The lesson

What happened. The same architecture trained under a Gaussian likelihood blurred two modes into their mean, 5. Under a mixture likelihood it would recover both.

Why it matters. Maximum likelihood under a Gaussian with fixed variance is least squares. Under a Bernoulli it is cross-entropy. The loss follows from the likelihood, not the other way round.

Principle. Match the likelihood to the data type first, then to the noise shape. Continuous pixels modeled as Bernoulli saturate. Discrete tokens modeled as Gaussian have the wrong units.

UNIT 04
0.90/0.31toy rate vs frontier rate, nats
Nugget 08 · U03-C11
The lesson

What happened. The toy paid 0.9013 nats for what the frontier sells at 0.3099. The gap is the family price.

Why it matters. Rate is the KL term, the latent information price. Distortion is the reconstruction error. The ELBO is rate plus distortion with a sign.

Principle. Reporting rate 0.9 nats as good without the distortion is meaningless. The pair (rate, distortion) is the point. And never compare VAE bound-BPD against flow exact-BPD: the comparison is rigged.
