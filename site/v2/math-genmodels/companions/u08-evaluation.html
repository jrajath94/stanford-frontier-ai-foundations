Chapter 1 · C01 to C02

The honest score

Source factU08-C01 The running toy: truth 0.5 N(-2, 0.25) + 0.5 N(2, 0.25). Model A: the moment-matched Gaussian N(0, 4.25), blurry but covering. Model B: N(2, 0.25), sharp but dropping the left mode. Model C: a memorizer that copies 50 training points. Held-out points: (-2.1, 1.9, -1.8, 2.2, 0.1). Key numbers: held-out loglik A = -10.1073, B = -70.949. Precision A = 0.4708, B = 0.9555. Recall A = 1.0, B = 0.5. 1-D FID(truth, A) = 0. Memorizer NN distance 0.0066 versus 0.4666.

Source factU08-C01 Model A wins by 60 nats. The point -2.1 decides it: A assigns -2.16 nats there, B assigns -33.85, the largest single gap (31.68 nats). At 0.1, A gives density 0.1933, B gives 0.0006. Per point: A averages -2.02 nats, B averages -14.19. The held-out set punishes the model that left probability mass off the data.

Source factU08-C02 Data point y = 1.0 under the model N(0, 4). Exact loglik: -1.7371. A VAE with approximate posterior q = N(0.5, 1) reports the ELBO: exact minus the gap KL(q||p) = 0.125, giving -1.8621. The VAE true likelihood could be -1.7371 (gap 0.125 of looseness) or -1.8621 (tight bound, worse model). The bound does not say which.

Illustration The bound is a floor. Drag the VAE gap and watch the bound move under the exact truth -1.7371. The lesson gap is 0.125. The comparison rule: exact against exact, or bound against bound with gaps labeled.

gap: 0.125

Nugget 01 · U08-C01

What happened. Five unseen points, 60 nats apart. One point, -2.1, contributed 31.68 of them.

Why it matters. Training likelihood always favors the more flexible model. Held-out likelihood favors the model that generalizes. The comparison needs the same held-out set, the same units, and exact numbers to be decisive.

Principle. Lock the held-out set away. Touch it once. Tuning on it turns it into a second training set and the score inflates.

Nugget 02 · U08-C02

What happened. Exact -1.7371, bound -1.8621, gap 0.125. A second VAE at bound -1.80 could be better or worse than the flow: its truth lies in [-1.80, infinity).

Why it matters. A bound is a one-sided promise. Comparing a bound against an exact number compares a floor against a value. Rankings need exact-versus-exact or bound-versus-bound with the gaps labeled.

Principle. Any comparison that ranks a bound against an exact number without labeling must be rejected.

Chapter 2 · C03 to C06

Samples under suspicion

Source factU08-C03 Radius 1.0 around each mode, 4,000 samples at seed 13 each. Model A: at least one sample lands within 1 of -2 and within 1 of 2. Coverage 2/2 = 1.0. Model B: all near 2. Coverage 1/2 = 0.5. The left mode is absent. No likelihood was needed: just samples and a ruler.

Source factU08-C04 Define the data manifold as within 1.0 of either mode. Model A: precision 0.4708 (its blur spreads samples off the manifold), recall 1.0. Model B: precision 0.9555 (sharp on its mode), recall 0.5.

One without the other lies: B is precise and incomplete, A is complete and sloppy.
U08-C04, on precision and recall

Source factU08-C05 The truth is bimodal with mean 0 and variance 4.25. Model A is N(0, 4.25): same mean, same variance, one hump. The 1-D FID: (0 - 0)^2 + (std diff)^2 = 0. Perfect score. The shapes share nothing beyond two moments.

Source factU08-C06 50 training points from the truth. Model C samples training points plus noise of scale 0.01. Mean nearest-neighbor distance to the training set: 0.0066. Model A samples: 0.4666. The memorizer histogram piles at zero. Likelihood cannot see it: a memorizer with slight smoothing scores held-out points near training points well.

Nugget 03 · U08-C03

What happened. Coverage 1.0 against 0.5, from 4,000 samples and a ruler. No density needed.

Why it matters. Mode coverage is a recall-style measure specialized to modes. It is crude and honest: it asks the one question likelihood averages away.

Principle. The radius must be smaller than the mode separation, or every model reads 1.0.

Nugget 04 · U08-C05

What happened. FID 0.0000. Two modes against one. The score was maximally wrong.

Why it matters. FID compares Gaussian fits to feature distributions: means and covariances only. Bimodality, skewness, tail shape, manifold structure are all invisible. Real FID adds two more failure layers: the features may miss what matters, and the Gaussian fit may miss the feature law.

Principle. Use FID for iteration, never for the final claim alone. Optimizing FID directly teaches the model the Inception features, not the data.

Nugget 05 · U08-C06

What happened. NN distance 0.0066 against 0.4666. The memorizer samples sat on top of the training data.

Why it matters. The nearest-neighbor test sees memorization directly: a pile at zero distance is the fingerprint. The test needs the training set, which external evaluators may not have: another reason to distrust single-number leaderboards.

Principle. A test set that overlaps the training set makes every model look like a memorizer. Deduplicate first.

Chapter 3 · C07 to C08

Fair fights

Source factU08-C07 Model A has 1,000 parameters, model B has 100,000. B wins on every metric. The comparison says nothing about the architectures: B had 100x the budget. The honest protocol fixes the budget: same parameters, same training flops, same sampling cost, then compare. The toy budget: 1,000 parameters, 10,000 training samples, 4,000 evaluation samples. The reported gaps (60 nats held-out, 0.5 recall) are then about the models, not the money.

Source factU08-C08 Draw 5,000 points from the known mixture. Fit the moment-matched Gaussian. Recovered mean -0.0317, variance 4.2352. Truth: 0 and 4.25. The standard error of the mean is sqrt(4.25/5000) = 0.029: the -0.0317 sits within 1.1 standard errors. Consistent. On real data this check is impossible: the truth is what the method must learn.

Nugget 06 · U08-C07

What happened. The big model won every metric with 100x the budget. The idea won nothing.

Why it matters. Compute matching separates ideas from resources. Match training compute, parameter count, and inference cost separately: a model can win training-matched and lose inference-matched. The tuning effort must be symmetric: asymmetric tuning is the most common cheat.

Principle. Both models must actually use the full budget. An under-tuned baseline is a strawman.

Nugget 07 · U08-C08

What happened. The recovered mean landed within 1.1 standard errors of the known truth. The check was exact because the experimenter wrote the truth.

Why it matters. Synthetic ground truth is the laboratory: bias, variance, coverage of intervals, all scored against the right answer. Methods graduate from synthetic to real only after passing here.

Principle. A method tuned on the synthetic truth exact form will not transfer. Keep the method blind to the truth parameters.

Chapter 4 · C09 to C12

The discipline

Source factU08-C09 Model A precision over 5 seeds (100-104): mean 0.4835, std 0.0115. The honest report: 0.484 +/- 0.012. A competing model at 0.490 is not better: the gap 0.0065 sits inside the noise. Without the error bar, the leaderboard lies.

Source factU08-C10 Claim: "Model A covers both modes." Pre-register: with 4,000 samples and radius 1.0, recall must be 1.0. Run: recall 1.0. The claim survives. The sharp version: "Model B covers both modes." Same protocol. Run: recall 0.5. The claim is falsified. The experiment had teeth because the failure criterion was written before the run.

Nugget 08 · U08-C10

What happened. Two pre-registered claims. A survived at recall 1.0. B fell at 0.5. The falsified claim was as informative as the surviving one.

Why it matters. A hypothesis without a failure criterion is a wish. Pre-registration blocks post-hoc storytelling: moving the goalposts after seeing the data. HARKing, hypothesizing after results are known, gives the test no power.

Principle. Write the claim, the metric, the threshold, and the sample size before the run. Report the outcome against the threshold. No re-runs until it passes.
