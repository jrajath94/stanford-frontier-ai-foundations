Chapter 1 · C01 to C02

Laws without formulas

Source factU05-C01 The running toy: real law p(x) = N(0, 1), model law q(x) = N(1, 1), test point x = 0. Key numbers: p(0) = 0.3989, q(0) = 0.2420, ratio r(0) = 1.6487, optimal discriminator D*(0) = 0.6225, JSD(p, q) = 0.1114, V* = -1.1635, W1 = 1.0.

Source factU05-C01 z ~ Uniform(-1, 1). Set g(z) = z^2 + 1. Every draw of z gives a draw of x in [1, 2]. The law of x has no clean density formula: the fold of the uniform under z^2 produces a curve that is easy to sample and painful to write. This is an implicit distribution: the law exists through the procedure that draws from it, not through a formula for p(x).

Source factU05-C02 The density ratio r(x) = p(x)/q(x) compares two laws point by point. At x = 0: r(0) = 0.3989/0.2420 = 1.6487. At x = 2: p(2) = 0.0540, q(2) = 0.2420, r(2) = 0.2231. The crossing point where r = 1 is x = 0.5, the midpoint of the means. Where r > 1, real data is more likely. Where r < 1, the model overproduces.

Nugget 01 · U05-C01

What happened. Three draws of z gave three valid samples in [1, 2]. No density was ever evaluated.

Why it matters. An implicit law is defined by a sampler, not by a density. Scoring p(x) for a given x is intractable in general. Asking an implicit model for p(x) has no answer.

Principle. Any likelihood reported for a GAN is a different object (a bound, a proxy) and must be labeled as such.

Nugget 02 · U05-C02

What happened. The ratio flipped from 1.6487 at x = 0 to 0.2231 at x = 2, crossing 1 at the midpoint 0.5.

Why it matters. The ratio is the whole story of the comparison: at each x, which side wins and by how much. Every f-divergence is an expectation of a function of r. Estimating the ratio is easier than estimating either density.

Principle. Model only the ratio when comparison is the goal. But where q has thinner tails than p, the ratio explodes in the tails and finite samples never see it.

Chapter 2 · C03 to C04

The best classifier

Source factU05-C03 Mix real and fake samples 50/50. The best classifier at point x reports the posterior that x is real: D*(x) = p(x) / (p(x) + q(x)). At x = 0: 0.3989 / (0.3989 + 0.2420) = 0.6225. At x = 2: 0.0540 / (0.0540 + 0.2420) = 0.1824. The discriminator is a ratio meter: D* = r/(1 + r).

Illustration The ratio meter. Move x and read p(x), q(x), the ratio r(x), and the optimal discriminator D*(x) for the lesson laws N(0,1) and N(1,1).

x: 0.00

Source factU05-C04 V(D, G) = E_{x~p}[log D(x)] + E_{z}[log(1 - D(g(z)))]. The discriminator maximizes V. The generator minimizes it. With the optimal D* plugged in, max_D V = -log 4 + 2 × JSD(p, q). On the toy: JSD = 0.1114, V* = -1.1635. The generator job reduces to minimization of the Jensen-Shannon divergence, but only at the discriminator optimum.

Nugget 03 · U05-C04

What happened. The game value split into -1.3863 plus 0.2228. At equilibrium p = q, D* = 0.5 everywhere, and neither player can improve.

Why it matters. Training alternates small steps instead of solving the inner max, so the practical objective is a moving target. The gap between the theory and the algorithm explains most GAN folklore.

Principle. Training G against a weak D optimizes an arbitrary moving target, not JSD. The loss curves mean nothing. Diagnose with held-out classifier accuracy, not with V.

Chapter 3 · C05

When divergences go blind

Source factU05-C05 p = N(0, 0.01), q = N(5, 0.01). The laws are disjoint. JSD = 0.6931 = log 2, its maximum. The discriminator is perfect. Move q a little closer, to N(4.9, 0.01): JSD is still 0.6931. The gradient of JSD with respect to the shift is zero. The generator learns nothing.

The loss is flat because the divergence is flat. No learning rate fixes geometry.
U05-C05, on JSD saturation

Nugget 04 · U05-C05

What happened. The means moved 0.1 closer. JSD did not move. Its gradient was exactly zero.

Why it matters. This is not a bug in the optimizer. It is the geometry of the divergence. When supports are disjoint, the mixture equals p/2 on the support of p and q/2 on the support of q, so JSD = log 2, constant in the parameters.

Principle. A discriminator at 100 percent on held-out real/fake pairs signals saturation, not success.

Chapter 4 · C06 to C08

Moving mass

Source factU05-C06 p is a point mass at 0. q is a point mass at 3. To reshape p into q, move the mass 3 units. The 1-Wasserstein distance is 3. For the running toy, W1(N(0,1), N(1,1)) = 1.0. Move q to N(2,1): W1 = 2.0. The distance is linear in the shift, with slope exactly 1.

Source factU05-C06 The Kantorovich dual: W1 = max_f E_p[f(x)] - E_q[f(x)] over 1-Lipschitz f. The discriminator becomes a critic with a slope limit, and the game regains its gradient.

Source factU05-C07 f(x) = x is 1-Lipschitz: the slope never exceeds 1. f(x) = 2x is 2-Lipschitz, not 1-Lipschitz. On the toy, f(x) = x gives E_p[f] - E_q[f] = 0 - 1 = -1. The absolute value 1 equals W1. f(x) = 2x gives -2, which exceeds the true distance: invalid critic, caught by the constraint.

Source factU05-C08 The gradient penalty replaces hard clipping with a soft term: lambda × E[(||grad f|| - 1)^2] on random interpolations between real and fake points, with lambda = 10. On the toy with f(x) = x: gradient norm 1, penalty 0, objective reads the true W1 = 1.0. A critic f(x) = 2x pays 10 × (2-1)^2 = 10 per point and gets suppressed.

Nugget 05 · U05-C06

What happened. Two disjoint laws still had a distance that shrank as they approached: 5.0 to 4.9 to 1.0.

Why it matters. Optimal transport measures the cheapest way to move mass, not the overlap of densities. This is the property JSD lacks.

Principle. Without the slope limit the dual is unbounded and training explodes. The constraint is the method: enforce it by clipping, penalty, or spectral normalization.

Chapter 5 · C09 to C12

Collapse and chaos

Source factU05-C09 The target is 0.5 N(-3, 1) + 0.5 N(3, 1). The generator outputs 0 for every z. All 2,000 samples sit at a single point. Left-mode recall: fraction of samples within 1 of -3 is 0.0 for the collapsed model. The target has about 0.35 of its mass there.

Source factU05-C11 The bilinear game min_x max_y x y with learning rate 0.1 from (1, 1): after 20 simultaneous steps the norm is 1.5622, up from 1.4142. The equilibrium at (0, 0) is correct and the algorithm never reaches it. The update eigenvalues are 1 +/- 0.1 i with magnitude sqrt(1.01) per step: rotation plus expansion.

Source factU05-C12 On the collapsed toy, precision is 1.0 (samples look plausible) and recall is 0.0 (the left mode is absent). One number would lie. The pair tells the truth. Inception Score can be gamed by adversarial examples for the classifier. FID misses everything beyond second moments.

Nugget 06 · U05-C09

What happened. Two thousand samples, one point. The discriminator never learned the valley between the modes.

Why it matters. The JSD objective does not forbid collapse strongly. The failure is visible only in the sample distribution, never in the loss.

Principle. Sharpness is precision. Collapse is a recall failure. One does not imply the other. Diagnose with held-out sample statistics, not the loss.

Nugget 07 · U05-C11

What happened. Twenty steps, norm 1.4142 to 1.5622. The iterates spiraled outward from the right answer.

Why it matters. The GAN game is not an optimization. It is a saddle-point problem, and simultaneous gradient steps on a saddle can diverge, cycle, or crawl. The Jacobian of the game has imaginary eigenvalues: rotation, not contraction.

Principle. In a game, one player loss can fall while the joint state diverges. Track a held-out metric, not the losses. The game needs algorithmic fixes, not smaller steps alone.

Nugget 08 · U05-C12

What happened. Three models, three rankings. A human ranked the sharp one first. A recall metric ranked the covering one first. A likelihood proxy ranked the memorizer first.

Why it matters. GAN evaluation is an open problem because the training objective is not a score. Every metric measures a projection. A model tuned to minimize FID learns the Inception features, not the data.

Principle. Pick the metric that punishes your failure mode. Report several. Distrust each.
