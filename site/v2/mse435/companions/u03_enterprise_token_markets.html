Section 1 · C01

Service as software: the 100x repricing

From the lessonC01 §2

One support ticket costs 1 hour of human time at 50 dollars per hour, or one AI task at 0.50 dollars. The task reprices by a factor of 100. Human cost is linear in tasks. AI cost is sublinear: fixed model cost spread over tasks plus tiny marginal compute.

From the lessonC01 §6

10,000 tickets per month: humans cost 500,000 dollars. AI costs 5,000 plus 20,000 of fixed integration and oversight, or 25,000. The saving is 475,000 per month. If the AI resolves only 70 percent and humans handle the rest: 3,500 plus 150,000 plus 20,000 is 173,500. Still a 65 percent saving, but the human tail dominates the cost.

Nugget 1 · From the lessonC01 §6

The task reprices 100x. The tail still matters. Human tickets cost 500,000 dollars per month. AI costs 25,000. But when the AI resolves only 70 percent, the human tail of 150,000 dominates the AI bill.

Why it matters companion
Averages hide the tail. The last 30 percent of hard tickets cost more than the first 70 percent of easy ones.
Transfer companion
Price the exception path, not the happy path. The tail sets the real unit cost.

Section 2 · C02

Integration: the model is 10.8 percent

From the lessonC02 §5

First-year total: 5,000 times 12 for the API, plus 150,000 data plumbing, 100,000 security review, 200,000 workflow change, 45,000 onboarding. Total 555,000 dollars. The model is 60,000 of it: 10.8 percent.

From the lessonC02 §6

Year two: 60,000 model plus 30,000 security annual plus 50,000 maintenance, or 140,000. Two-year total 695,000, of which the model is 120,000, or 17 percent. Integration dominates early, then fades. The decision metric is the multi-year total, not the API bill.

Nugget 2 · From the lessonC02 §5

Budget the car, not the engine. The model is 10.8 percent of the first-year cost. Anyone who budgets the API bill and forgets the integration is off by 9x.

Why it matters companion
Software cost is mostly connection cost: plumbing, security, workflow change, onboarding. The model is the smallest line on the invoice.
Transfer companion
In every AI business case, list integration lines before model lines. The big number decides.

Section 3 · C03

Open or closed: the stack flips with volume

From the lessonC03 §5

Open stack: 30,000 dollars per month to host plus 200,000 integration. Two-year cost 920,000. Closed API: 40,000 per month plus 50,000 integration. Two-year cost 1,010,000. Open wins by 90,000 before any control premium.

From the lessonC03 §6

Cut volume in half. Open hosting falls only to 20,000 per month, fixed-heavy: 680,000. Closed API falls to 20,000 per month, pure variable: 530,000. Closed wins by 150,000.

The stack choice flips with volume, exactly like build vs lease.

From the lessonC03 §6 Direct quote from the lesson page.

Nugget 3 · From the lessonC03 §6

Fixed-heavy loses at low volume. Variable wins there. At full volume open wins by 90,000. At half volume closed wins by 150,000. The same two stacks, opposite winners.

Why it matters companion
Hosting is fixed-heavy: it cannot shrink with demand. The API is pure variable: it shrinks exactly with demand. Volume decides, not philosophy.
Transfer companion
Match cost structure to demand certainty. Uncertain volume rents variable cost. Certain high volume buys fixed cost.

Section 4 · C04

Compute supply: one line, 3.672T tokens a day

From the lessonC04 §5

One serving line: 1,000 GPUs at 50 tokens per second at 85 percent utilization. Tokens per day: 1,000 times 50 times 0.85 times 86,400, or 3.672 trillion. In 1M-token blocks: 3,672,000 blocks per day.

From the lessonC04 §6

At 4 dollars per 1M tokens, daily revenue capacity is 14.688M dollars. Fleet cost at 1.08 dollars per GPU-hour: 25,920 dollars per day. The margin is enormous, which is why serving is attractive and why competition attacks the price.

Section 5 · C05

Token demand: the shock triples revenue

From the lessonC05 §5

Token demand follows the U01 market. Before the shock: P = 16, Q = 68. Revenue 1,088,000 dollars per day. After: P = 28, Q = 104. Revenue 2,912,000 per day.

From the lessonC05 §6

The shock nearly triples daily revenue. The price rises 75 percent and quantity rises 53 percent. The steeper the supply, the more the shock lands on quantity and the less on price. That is why suppliers race to add the serving line from C04.

Section 6 · C06

Capacity bottlenecks: the minimum decides

From the lessonC06 §5

A serving pipeline has three stages at 100, 40, 80 requests per second. Rate is the minimum: 40 rps. Utilizations: 40, 100, 50 percent. The batcher binds. Money spent raising stage 1 or 3 is wasted until the batcher moves.

From the lessonC06 §6

Upgrade the batcher from 40 to 90 rps for 200,000 dollars. New rate: minimum of 100, 90, 80, or 80 rps. Throughput doubles. Spend another 200,000 on the batcher, to 130: rate stays 80. Zero gain. The second 200,000 belongs on stage 3.

Nugget 4 · From the lessonC06 §6

Feed the binding stage. Starve the rest. 200,000 dollars on the batcher doubled throughput from 40 to 80 rps. The next 200,000 on the same stage bought zero. The bottleneck moved, and the money did not follow it.

Why it matters companion
A serial line runs at its minimum. Every dollar above the minimum is waste until the minimum moves.
Transfer companion
Find the minimum, spend there, re-measure. Repeat. Optimization without re-measurement funds the wrong stage.

Section 7 · C07

Inference versus tuition: the model rent

From the lessonC07 §5

Tuition costs 5M dollars. Rent costs 4 dollars per 1M tokens. Unit cost is tuition amortized over lifetime tokens plus rent. At 50B lifetime tokens: 100 plus 4, or 104 dollars per 1M. At 500B: 10 plus 4, or 14.

From the lessonC07 §6

Volume grew 10x. Unit cost fell 7.4x. The crossover where tuition equals rent: 1.25T lifetime tokens. Below it, tuition dominates. Above it, rent dominates. Most successful models cross it. Most failed ones never get near.

Section 8 · C08

Long contracts: the path decides the winner

From the lessonC08 §5

A three-year contract at 3 dollars against market prices 4, 3, 2. Volume 120 blocks per year. Market PV at 10 percent: 914.2 dollars. Contract PV: 895.3. The contract wins by 18.9 dollars.

From the lessonC08 §6

Make the market fall faster, 4, 2, 1: market PV 724.8. The contract loses by 170.5 dollars. The sign of the deal flips on the price path, not on the headline 3 dollars.

Nugget 5 · From the lessonC08 §6

Contracts bet on the price path. The same 3-dollar contract wins by 18.9 against a slow price fall and loses by 170.5 against a fast one. The headline price never decided anything.

Why it matters companion
A fixed price is a forecast in disguise. It wins exactly when the market falls slower than the contract assumed.
Transfer companion
Never sign a fixed price without a price-path scenario. Model the path, then decide.

Section 9 · C09

Ownership: rent wins on cost, own can win on lift

From the lessonC09 §5

Own: 2M build plus 300,000 per year to run. Rent: 500,000 per year. The annuity 3 years at 10 percent is 2.4869. Own PV 2,746,055. Rent PV 1,243,426. Rent wins by 1.5M on cost alone.

From the lessonC09 §6

Add differentiation: the owned model lifts conversion 2 percent on 50M revenue, or 1M per year. PV of the lift is 2.4869M. Own net cost 0.259M against rent 1.243M. Own wins once differentiation is counted. Cost-only ownership math systematically favors renting.

Section 10 · C10

Data governance: price the fence and the breach

From the lessonC10 §5

A data fence costs resolution: 20 points on 10,000 tickets is 2,000 extra misses at 50 dollars, or 100,000 dollars per month. The breach it prevents: 1 percent annual chance times 5M fine is 50,000 per year, or 4,167 per month. On expected value the fence costs 24x its benefit.

From the lessonC10 §6

Add reputation: one breach costs 50M in lost trust. Expected 41,667 per month. Still below 100,000, but the same order of magnitude. The decision turns on the tail number. Governance is argued in scenarios, not means.

Section 11 · C11

Unit prices: the stack and the break-even

From the lessonC11 §5

A token price of 4.00 dollars decomposes: 2.50 cost plus 1.00 margin plus 0.50 buffer. Margin rate 25 percent. The decomposition is a diagnostic: when a competitor prices at 2.00, either their cost is lower or the low price is a loss leader.

From the lessonC11 §6

Break-even for a 1M monthly fixed cost: contribution is 1.50 per block. Needed volume 666,667 blocks per month. Below that, the 4-dollar price loses money despite the margin.

Section 12 · C12

Sensitivity: the tornado ranks your attention

From the lessonC12 §5

Four drivers move the token price. Swings: utilization 2.40, power price 1.60, batch size 1.20, chip cost 1.00 dollars. Rank: utilization first, then power, batch, chip.

From the lessonC12 §6

The honest read needs a correction: utilization is the top single driver but not bigger than power and chip together, 2.60. Fix utilization first, then power. The one-at-a-time ranking ignores interactions.

Nugget 6 · From the lessonC12 §5

Rank attention by swing. Utilization moves the price 2.40 dollars across its range: more than power, batch, or chip alone. If you can negotiate one input, negotiate scheduling and batching policy.

Why it matters companion
Attention is scarce. The tornado allocates it to the driver with the biggest swing.
Transfer companion
Tornado-chart every model you defend. Then work the top bar first.
