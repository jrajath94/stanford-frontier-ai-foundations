Concept 01 · Index build versus query

The catalog and its price From the lesson

Compiling a library catalog costs a fortune, while consulting it costs pennies. The build is a one-time fixed cost B, and every query pays the marginal cost q plus its fraction of B. At N queries the amortized cost is B/N + q, so each query gets cheaper as volume grows.

Toy: 1M documents, one 7200 s build, 0.005 s per query. At N = 1M the build adds 7200/1e6 = 0.0072 s = 7.2 ms per query, 12.2 ms total. At N = 1000 the build adds 7.2 s per query, swamping everything, so skip the build. Against a 50 ms brute-force scan, indexing wins once B/N + q drops below 0.05, which needs N over 7200/0.045 = 160,000 queries. Ten queries a day never earns an index, while a million queries do. companion Rebuilds restart the clock: a news index rebuilt hourly must re-earn itself hourly.

Nugget 1

Divide the fixed cost by the volume.

Why it matters companion
12.2 ms per query at a million queries versus 7.2 s per query at a thousand: identical index, opposite verdicts. Volume decides whether the build is an investment or a waste.
Transfer
Any fixed-cost decision reduces to B/N. Estimate N before you approve B.
Concept 02 · Embedding storage

The warehouse of vectors From the lesson

A million documents, each with a 768-dim vector: what does that occupy? Dense fp32 stacks full pallets: 1e6 x 768 x 4 = 3,072,000,000 bytes = 2.86 GiB, quick to fetch, enormous to store. Product quantization at 64 bytes per vector vacuum-seals each one: 1e6 x 64 = 64,000,000 bytes = 61 MiB, 48x smaller, but every fetch must unseal its vector at query time.

Sealed vectors give approximate distances: recall slips, and C03 measures how far. At 100M documents, dense needs 286 GiB (a dedicated server) while PQ fits in 6.1 GiB of RAM. Choose PQ when RAM binds, dense when recall is paramount, and fp16 dense as the middle option that halves the footprint exactly. companion Seal too aggressively on a hard distractor set and recall collapses: the top results come back wrong.

Storage lab. Illustration Set the corpus size and vector width. The lab prices dense fp32 against 64-byte PQ.


Default: the lesson toy, 1M docs at 768 dims.

Nugget 2

Vacuum-pack the vectors, budget the unpacking.

Why it matters companion
48x smaller turns a server-class problem into a RAM-class one. Sealing happens once, but unsealing happens per query, and over-sealing breaks recall.
Transfer
Sealing trades a one-time saving for a per-use cost. Count both sides of it.
Concept 03 · ANN recall and latency

The phone book with tabs From the lesson

Exact search is slow, so approximate nearest neighbors sacrifice a little recall for a lot of speed. IVF partitions the space with a coarse quantizer and searches only nearby partitions. HNSW navigates a graph of shortcut edges toward the query. Both shortcuts cost some accuracy: the true neighbor sometimes sits just outside the searched region.

Toy table, labeled a toy and not a benchmark: brute force reaches recall 1.00 at 50 ms. IVF reaches 0.95 at 5 ms, a 10x speedup, missing 5 in 100. HNSW reaches 0.98 at 8 ms, 6.25x faster. Per recall point, IVF delivers 0.95 in 5 ms (0.19 per ms) and HNSW delivers 0.98 in 8 ms (0.1225 per ms), so IVF is cheaper per point here. Impose a recall floor of 0.97 and only HNSW clears it among the two: the floor selects the system. The dials are nprobe and efSearch, trading more work for more recall. companion The mean hides a tail: hard queries with rare terms can sit at 0.70 while the mean reads 0.97.

Nugget 3

The floor picks the system.

Why it matters companion
Cheapest-per-point and floor-qualified crown different winners. State the recall floor first, then pick the fastest system above it.
Transfer
Requirements before optimization: filter on the constraint, then minimize the cost.
Concept 04 · Hybrid retrieval

Two witnesses From the lesson

BM25 matches keywords while dense vectors match meaning. Merging their lists needs no score calibration when you fuse ranks instead. Reciprocal rank fusion gives each system a vote of 1/(k + rank): high ranks count heavily, deep ranks barely count, and rank order is the shared language. With k = 60 softening the differences, doc D at rank 3 in BM25 and rank 5 in dense scores 1/63 + 1/65 = 0.015873 + 0.015385 = 0.031258.

A doc ranked 1st and 100th scores 1/61 + 1/160 = 0.016393 + 0.00625 = 0.022643 and loses to D. Two middling ranks defeat one stellar plus one terrible rank, because fusion rewards consensus. Hybrid shines exactly where the systems agree, which is where each alone feels uncertain. The ranks must stay comparable, though: a list of 10 against a list of 1000 makes rank 50 mean different things and skews the fusion. companion Consensus is the signal, and a lone top rank is a gamble.

Fusion lab. Illustration Set two ranks and the damping k. The lab scores the fusion and tests agreement against a lone star.


Default: the lesson toy, ranks 3 and 5 at k = 60.

Nugget 4

Ranks are the common tongue.

Why it matters companion
Two systems with incompatible scores still share ranks. Fusion needs no calibration meeting, only the formula.
Transfer
When combining judges that use different scales, convert to ranks before averaging.
Concept 05 · Filtering and reranking

Airport security From the lesson

The cross-encoder is accurate but slow, so the cascade screens cheaply first. The ANN sweeps the full corpus in milliseconds, and only the survivors reach the slow, careful reranker. Total cost = cheap x many + expensive x few, and the funnel shape drives the entire economy.

Toy: 1M docs, ANN top 100 in 5 ms, filter to top 20, cross-encoder scores the top 5 at 20 ms each. Total: 5 + 20 x 20 = 405 ms, with 99% of it in the reranker. Skip the ANN and the bill is 1M x 20 ms: absurd. Tighten the funnel to the top 10: 5 + 200 = 205 ms, half the cost, but recall may slip (C03). The reranker owns the bill, so optimize it first through distillation or quantization (unit 05). The funnel's risk: if the ANN's top 100 misses the gold document at recall 0.95, the reranker never receives it and the answer comes back wrong. companion Every narrowing of the funnel trades recall for latency.

Nugget 5

The last stage sets the bill.

Why it matters companion
405 ms with 400 in the reranker makes ANN tuning noise. Optimize the funnel's narrow end first.
Transfer
In any pipeline, the stage that is expensive and small dominates. Find it before tuning anything.
Concept 06 · Cluster economics guests

Field reports, not textbooks From the lesson

A guest lecture is a dispatch from the field, not a textbook chapter. The skill is evidence grading: what got measured, what got assumed, what has gone stale. The arithmetic is the first check: price x count x time. When the product matches the claim, you have internal consistency at least.

Toy: a guest says 1000 GPUs cost $72k a day. GPU price $3/hour, a labeled hypothetical, never a quote. Bill = 1000 x 24 x 3 = $72,000/day. At 48% MFU (unit 09), useful work costs $72k / 0.48 = $150k per useful-day. A 30-day run: $2.16M gross, $4.5M per useful-month. The guest's number survives the arithmetic check, but its grade hinges on whether $3 was measured or merely stated, and on its date. A 2022 price cited in 2024 gives correct arithmetic with a wrong conclusion. companion Never accept a guest number at face value. Grade it, then cite it.

Nugget 6

Multiply, then grade.

Why it matters companion
The arithmetic takes ten seconds and catches internal inconsistency. The grading takes longer and catches the rest: staleness, hidden assumptions, bias.
Transfer
Every cited number gets two checks: does the math close, and is the source still fresh.
Concept 07 · SSM guest evidence

Sort the claims, do not swallow them From the lesson

Architecture claims from a guest fall into four grades. Measured means a table with baselines and seeds sits in front of you. Derived means a complexity claim you can verify yourself (unit 07 derives O(T)). Stated means the speaker's own summary of their work. Opinion means a forecast about the future. The ranking runs measured, then derived, then stated, then opinion.

Toy: claim A, two times faster, with no table behind it: STATED, so never size a cluster on it. Claim B, O(T d^2): DERIVED, safe for planning. Claim C, the future belongs to SSMs: OPINION, interesting, weight zero in engineering decisions. Never upgrade a grade without the artifact: only the table's arrival moves a claim from stated to measured. The speaker's position matters too: someone who runs an SSM company earns extra skepticism on stated claims, not less. companion Cite the artifact, not the speaker.

Concept 08 · Project proposal and milestone

The contract From the lesson

A proposal is a contract spelling out your planned attempt, your falsification condition, and your budget. The milestone audits progress: what ran, what broke, what changed. The graders hunt for falsifiability, and a proposal with no possible failure cannot clear the bar.

Toy rubric, five criteria at 2 points each: question, hypothesis, baselines, budget, and kill conditions. A toy proposal scores [2, 2, 1, 2, 0] = 7/10. The zero lands on failure criteria, whose line is the most important one, and it is absent. The call: revise and resubmit. Add one kill line, abandoning X if metric Y does not move by Z: +2, total 9/10. A 10/10 proposal with no results still beats a 7/10, because the contract outweighs early numbers. The classic failure: the baseline outperforms the idea in week 2, and without failure criteria the team rationalizes instead of pivoting. companion Writing the contract costs hours, while skipping it costs the project.

Nugget 8

A proposal with no failure mode cannot pass.

Why it matters companion
The failure criteria are the only line protecting you when the baseline wins in week 2. Without them, sunk cost writes the next plan.
Transfer
Every plan needs a pre-written exit. Fix the kill condition before you get invested.
Concept 09 · Profiling hypothesis

Predict first From the lesson

Profiling begins with a written prediction, not with a tool. The hypothesis identifies the suspect, the mechanism, and the expected number, and the profiler then confirms or kills it. Profiling without a hypothesis is a fishing expedition: you will catch something, and it will mislead you.

Toy hypothesis: 62% of the step goes to attention, because T=8192 makes the scores move 256 MiB of traffic. Measured: 58%. The 4-point error lets the hypothesis survive. Had the profiler reported 12%, the hypothesis would die and the real suspect, say dispatch, would take over. The poster reports both figures, predicted 62 against measured 58. Honest deltas beat exact hits. companion The hypothesis is what gives the measurement meaning.

Nugget 9

The prediction gives the measurement meaning.

Why it matters companion
A measured 58% is trivia without the predicted 62%. Against the prediction, the 4-point gap is evidence that your mental model works.
Transfer
Write the number down before you measure. Surprise shows only against a prediction.
Concept 10 · Reproducible optimization

Rumors and evidence From the lesson

A claim of improvement with no seeds is a rumor. Three seeds form the minimum unit of evidence: a mean plus a spread. Calling it faster requires the gap to clear the spread, on identical hardware, under an identical budget.

Toy: losses [2.31, 2.29, 2.33]. Mean = 6.93/3 = 2.31. Deviations [0, -0.02, 0.02] give a sum of squares 0.0008, variance 0.0008/2 = 0.0004, std 0.02. A rival at 2.30 leaves a gap of 0.01, under one std: no evidence. The trap: run the seeds on mixed GPUs with nondeterministic kernels, and the spread measures the hardware rather than the idea. companion Seeds multiply the bill by n, while rumors stay cheap and worthless.

Nugget 10

One seed is a rumor.

Why it matters companion
The rival wins by 0.01 against a spread of 0.02. Without seeds you ship noise as a finding.
Transfer
Never accept a comparison without its spread. The gap must clear the noise.
Concept 11 · Quality regression

The gate From the lesson

Speed with no quality gate slides downhill fast. The gate is a contract: nothing ships when the task metric drops more than epsilon. Delta = (new - old)/old on the real task score, and the candidate ships when delta reaches at least -epsilon. The metric must be the genuine one, not a proxy the optimization can game: a perplexity gate passes while task quality falls anyway.

Toy: epsilon 0.5%. Candidate A at -0.3% passes. Candidate B at -0.7% fails, no ship. A third candidate at exactly -0.5% passes, since the gate uses >=. The gate is one-sided: small regressions pass as noise while large ones do not. Epsilon comes from seed noise (C10): here 0.5% sits above typical noise. companion The gate costs one eval run per candidate, while skipping it costs the product.

Gate lab. Illustration Set the quality delta and the epsilon. The lab rules on the ship.


Default: the lesson toy, A at -0.3% against epsilon 0.5%.

Nugget 11

No ship without the gate.

Why it matters companion
Twice as fast with -0.7% quality is a product regression in a speedup costume. The gate is the only thing that sees through it.
Transfer
Every optimization needs its guardrail metric decided in advance. Speed is never the only axis.
Concept 12 · Operational defense

No bluffing From the lesson

The defense is a live exam on your own work. Five questions probe the load-bearing walls: the claim, the proof, the baseline, the failure modes, the cost. Saying you do not know is acceptable. Bluffing is not.

Toy rubric, 2 points each: the defense scores [2, 2, 1, 2, 1] = 8/10. The baseline and cost answers came back thin. Before the session, redo the baseline right and price the run. A 10/10 defense on a failed experiment outscores an 8/10 on a lucky one, because honesty is the scoring axis. Negative results defend well: it failed, here is the proof, here is what it eliminates. The skeptic will ask for the baseline you skipped, and the poster folds. companion Rehearse with a hostile friend. Every rehearsal surfaces one gap.

Nugget 12

Honesty is the scoring axis.

Why it matters companion
A failed experiment defended honestly outscores a lucky one defended thinly. The defense grades the engineer, not the luck involved.
Transfer
In any review, the admission you volunteer beats the gap they uncover.
Synthesis

The arc of the unit companion

The unit runs one arc: retrieve, then prove. First make retrieval cheap: amortize the index (C01), pack the vectors (C02), pick the ANN by the recall floor (C03), fuse the witnesses (C04), and narrow the funnel (C05). Then make the claims believable: grade every number you did not measure yourself (C06, C07), write the contract before the work (C08), predict before you profile (C09), seed every claim (C10), gate every speedup on quality (C11), and defend it all without bluffing (C12).

The through-line is simple: cheap is easy to claim and hard to prove. The first half of the unit buys speed, and the second half buys the right to say so.
