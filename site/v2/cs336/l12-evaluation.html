Original lecture. Percy Liang tours evaluation: perplexity, exams, chat, agents, reasoning, safety, and validity.

How to read this lesson

Data shapes the model, and next week is data. But first: what behavior do we want? Level 1 (Core): what “good” means, perplexity, the exam treadmill. Level 2 (Deep): chat eval, agents, ARC, safety, validity, and the philosophy.

Level 1: What is good?

Four lenses, no correct answer 02:38 ⏱02:38. Benchmarks (Artificial Analysis intelligence index). Cost (intelligence vs inference price: correlated, not aligned). Preference (Arena: which answer people like). Usage (OpenRouter: what people pay for). The deep point: evaluation sets North Stars, and North Stars shape what gets built 01:32 ⏱01:32.

Evaluation turns an abstract construct (reasoning, conversation) into a concrete metric over concrete prompts 02:01 ⏱02:01. That translation is the whole challenge.

Level 1: Perplexity

A language model is a distribution P(x). Perplexity asks how much mass it puts on the test set 05:30 ⏱05:30. The 2010s paradigm: in-distribution, train split to test split, PTB and WikiText-103. GPT-2 (2019) broke it: train on WebText, evaluate zero-shot on everyone else’s benchmarks. Out-of-distribution became the norm 07:52 ⏱07:52.

“Perplexity is all you need”: the unique minimizer is the true distribution, and the true distribution solves everything 09:46 ⏱09:46. Belief in this drove scaling before the gains were obvious.

Catches. Perplexity charges bits for boring tokens (“founded” vs “1885”). Conditional perplexity focuses on the tokens that matter 11:43 ⏱11:43. Cloze benchmarks are perplexity in disguise: LAMBADA and HellaSwag are next-token prediction with carefully chosen positions 13:22 ⏱13:22. And perplexity leaderboards need trust: un-normalized logprobs cheat, so the probabilities must be real 15:18 ⏱15:18. Downstream tasks are black-box and checkable. Perplexity is white-box and trust-based.

If perplexity is the true objective, why do we need any other benchmark?

Three reasons. First, perplexity spends capacity on every token equally, including tokens nobody cares about. Conditional perplexity and task benchmarks focus the measurement. Second, upstream-to-downstream transfer is uncertain (Lecture 9): a better perplexity does not guarantee a better assistant. Third, perplexity cannot be checked black-box. You must trust the reported probabilities. Benchmarks exist because the cleanest metric is not always the most honest or the most relevant one. It changed the contract. In-distribution evaluation rewards memorizing one dataset’s quirks. Zero-shot rewards a model that generalizes to tasks it never trained on. That reframing, from “fit this distribution” to “handle anything,” is what turned language models from research artifacts into general-purpose tools.

Level 1: The exam treadmill

Exams give control over subject and difficulty, with unambiguous answers 18:18 ⏱18:18. MMLU (2020): 57 subjects, few-shot, radical at the time. GPT-3 barely above chance on small models, now 90s 19:07 ⏱19:07. MMLU-Pro: 10 choices, chain of thought, 33 to 88. GPQA: PhD contractors, diamond validation, experts at 65%, models now at 94 23:20 ⏱23:20. Humanity’s Last Exam: crowdsourced, private held-out set, Mythos at 64.7: still hard 27:29 ⏱27:29.

Multiple choice survives: it can be arbitrarily hard. Its limit is realism: nobody asks HLE questions except on HLE 30:03 ⏱30:03. And contamination shadows every number: the test set lives on the internet, and so does the training data 26:14 ⏱26:14.

Level 2: Chat evaluation

Open-ended questions have no ground truth. Chatbot Arena: two anonymized responses, humans pick, ELO ranking 32:58 ⏱32:58. Virtues: real prompts, sparse comparisons, natural updating. Vices: unknown rater distribution, style conflated with correctness, sycophancy rewarded, judges who do not know the answer judging correctness 35:52 ⏱35:52.

AlpacaEval: LLM judge vs a baseline, win rate. The length bias got gamed, then debiased 38:26 ⏱38:26. WildBench: per-prompt checklists make judging well-defined 41:39 ⏱41:39. Always evaluate the metric: AlpacaEval correlated 0.98 with Arena, on the models of that era 40:36 ⏱40:36. Pairwise beats absolute. Rubrics beat vibes.

Level 2: Agent evaluation

Evaluate what the model does, not what it says. SWE-bench: fix the GitHub issue, pass the tests. 16% to 93% (Verified cleaned the bad tasks) 46:01 ⏱46:01. Terminal-Bench: a terminal, crowdsourced tasks, hours to weeks. CyBench: 40 CTFs, now solved. MLE-bench: Kaggle competitions.

The scaffold is half the score: same model, different agent, different accuracy 48:57 ⏱48:57. Planning, delegation, memory, context engineering: all tuned per model. And benchmarks need audits: an empty response scored 38% on TorchBench. Docent inspects traces for such pathologies 74:50 ⏱74:50.

Level 2: ARC and pure reasoning

ARC-AGI (2019): grids solvable by humans in seconds, designed so knowledge does not help 53:31 ⏱53:31. GPT-3: zero. Then o1/o3: ARC-1 solved, ARC-2 nearly, ARC-3 (interactive) now the frontier with low scores 56:28 ⏱56:28. Caveat: “pure” reasoning may not exist, and the benchmark is human-bounded, not superhuman 58:16 ⏱58:16.

Level 2: Safety

HarmBench: harmful prompts, expect refusal 61:05 ⏱61:05. AIR-Bench: taxonomy from EU, China, and US regulations 61:38 ⏱61:38. Jailbreaking (GCG) optimizes past refusals, and attacks transfer from open to closed models 62:28 ⏱62:28. Safety is contextual: politics, law, norms vary. Risks vary too: hallucinations, sycophancy, crime, lost critical thinking. Dual use cuts both ways: the hacking agent is also the pentesting agent 64:48 ⏱64:48.

Level 2: Validity

Ecological validity: does the eval match real use? GDPVal: professionals across GDP sectors writing real tasks 66:00 ⏱66:00. Clinical tasks from 29 clinicians, not med-school exams 67:11 ⏱67:11. Claude usage analysis: let models summarize real query patterns, privately 67:39 ⏱67:39.

Scientific validity: contamination and quality.

  • Detect: question-order preference betrays memorization 69:50 ⏱69:50.
  • Report: norms demanding no-train-on-test justification 70:26 ⏱70:26.
  • Fresh evals: LiveCodeBench scrapes past the cutoff 73:31 ⏱73:31.
  • Private evals: internal code, rejected papers, HLE’s held-out 73:17 ⏱73:17.

Dataset quality needs audits: SWE-bench Verified, MMLU cleanups, and always look at the outputs yourself 74:36 ⏱74:36.

Your model scores 94 on GPQA. Should you celebrate?

Cautiously. First, contamination: the questions live on the internet, and contamination is subtle (sources, not just the test set). Second, saturation dynamics: GPQA went from 39% (GPT-4) to 94%, so the benchmark is near its ceiling and discriminates poorly at the top. Third, purpose: GPQA measures exam QA, not your deployment. Celebrate the HLE number more, audit for contamination always, and check the capability you actually ship. When the benchmark is both high-stakes and public. Public benchmarks get trained on, deliberately or via data leakage, and their signal decays. A private eval (internal code, unpublished writing, held-out sets) preserves the signal because the model cannot have seen it. The cost is maintenance: you must keep it private and refresh it. For purchase decisions and safety claims, that cost is worth it.

Level 2: The philosophy

No one evaluation rules all 75:41 ⏱75:41. Purpose picks the benchmark: buying (ecological validity), measuring intelligence (hard exams, ARC), improving the model (perplexity), shipping safely (safety benches). And we evaluate models and systems now, not methods: anything goes, which is why the shipped artifact is what matters 76:45 ⏱76:45. Declare the purpose first.

Recap: the whole lesson on one screen

1. Define good first

Benchmarks, cost, preference, usage. Evaluation sets North Stars. The star you pick shapes what gets built.

Key: purpose precedes metric

2. Perplexity is the root

P(x) on the test set. Best is the entropy of truth. Charges for boring tokens. Leaderboards need trust.

Key: mass on the test set

3. Exams saturate

MMLU to HLE: each harder, each with a shelf life. Multiple choice survives. Realism does not.

Key: shelf lives

4. Judge the judge

Arena: pairwise ELO, real prompts, unknown raters. AlpacaEval: LLM judge, debiased. Checklists scope judgment.

Key: pairwise beats absolute

5. Agents do things

SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench, CyBench: checkable outcomes. The scaffold is half the score. Audit the traces.

Key: scaffold counts

6. Isolate reasoning

ARC: knowledge-free grids. Reasoning models solved ARC-1. ARC-3 is the new frontier. Human-bounded.

Key: reasoning vs knowledge

7. Assume the worst

Detect order effects, report overlap, refresh evals, keep some private. Contamination is subtle.

Key: trust, then verify

8. Purpose picks the bench

Buy, measure, improve, ship: different goals, different evals. We evaluate shipped systems now, not methods.

Key: declare the purpose
