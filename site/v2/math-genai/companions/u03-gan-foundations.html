Opening

Two painters, one judge

The running toy: p_data = N(0, 1). The generator maps noise z to x = mu_g + sig_g z with z ~ N(0, 1), so p_g = N(mu_g, sig_g). From the lesson C01-C12 The bad start: mu_g = 1.0, sig_g = 0.5. The collapse toy (C06, C12): p_data = 0.5 N(−2, 1) + 0.5 N(2, 1), collapsed generator p_g = N(2, 1). The conditional toy (C08): class 0 lives at −2, class 1 at 2.

The arc: two players fight over one value function (C01-C02). The best referee has a closed form (C03), and with it the game is exactly JS minimization (C04). Practice breaks the theory in three precise ways: saturation (C05), mode collapse (C06), and three gradient pathologies (C07). Then control: steer it (C08), scale it (C09), invert it (C10), balance it (C11), and score it honestly (C12).

C01 · Generator and discriminator

Two players, opposite goals

We want samples from p_data but have no formula for it. From the lesson C01 Two nets play. The generator G takes noise z and outputs a fake x. The discriminator D takes an x and outputs one number in (0, 1): its bet that x came from the data pile. Likelihood is unavailable: p_data has no formula, and p_g has no tractable density either. The game replaces it.

The value function: From the lesson C01 V(D, G) = E over p_data of log D(x) + E over noise of log(1 − D(G(z))). D pushes V up (call out fakes, trust real data). G pushes V down (make fakes that D trusts). Same V, opposite goals: a two-player zero-sum game.

At the bad start, a perfect referee gives D*(0) = 0.7870, D*(1) = 0.2327, D*(2) = 0.3333. From the lesson C01 The referee is confident near 0 (data country) and near 1 (fake country). The failure case: a generator that ignores z (constant output). Then D learns D = 1 except at that point, and G never learns. If the outputs of G have zero variance, the game is already broken.

Nugget 01 · C01

The game replaces the likelihood.

What happened
With no density for p_data and no tractable density for p_g, the lesson drops likelihood entirely and scores the generator through a referee instead.
Why it matters
This is the fundamental trade of the unit: samples without densities. Everything downstream (sharp images, no convergence theory, the whole pathology zoo) follows from what was given up here.
Transferable principle
When the direct score is unavailable, score through an adversary. companion A learned judge is weaker than a formula but stronger than nothing.

C02 · The minimax objective

The inner max makes the number informative

min_G max_D V(D, G). Read inside out. From the lesson C02 For a fixed G, D plays its best: max_D V. That gives a number that depends on G only: the value of the game against the best referee. Then G minimizes that number over generators. The max must be inside: D must be the best response to the current G, or G trains against a lazy referee and learns nothing.

At the bad start, max_D V = −1.3537 bits. From the lesson C02 If D is lazy (D = 0.5 everywhere), V = −2.0 regardless of G: G gets zero signal about where its mass is wrong. The Nash equilibrium: p_g = p_data and D(x) = 1/2 everywhere, with V = log2(1/2) + log2(1/2) = −2 bits.

The failure case: update G against a frozen random D. From the lesson C02 G collapses its mass onto whatever the random D already trusts. The inner max must track G.

Nugget 02 · C02

The inner max makes the number informative.

What happened
A lazy referee scores every generator −2.0. Only the best-response referee (−1.3537 at the bad start) tells G where its mass is wrong.
Why it matters
Adversarial training is only as good as the adversary. An undertrained critic does not give a gentler signal. It gives no signal.
Transferable principle
Judge against the strongest fair opponent you can afford. companion A weak judge flatters. A strong judge teaches.

C03 · The optimal discriminator

The best referee is a ratio

Fix G, so p_g is fixed. From the lesson C03 For each x, maximize p_data(x) log D(x) + p_g(x) log(1 − D(x)) over D(x) in (0, 1). Derivative: a/y − b/(1−y) = 0 with a = p_data(x), b = p_g(x), so y = a/(a+b). The formula is forced by the derivative, not guessed:

D*_G(x) = p_data(x) / (p_data(x) + p_g(x))

The optimal discriminator is the Bayes posterior: the probability that x came from the data pile given equal pile sizes. From the lesson C03 This is exactly the classifier of U02-C04. The GAN discriminator is a density-ratio estimator wearing a game-theory costume. On the toy, at x = 0: a = 0.3990, b = 0.1079, y = 0.7870. At x = 2: a = 0.0540, b = 0.1080, ratio 1/3 = 0.3333.

Nugget 03 · C03

The best referee is a ratio.

What happened
Pointwise maximization of the value function forced D*(x) = p_data/(p_data + p_g): the perfect judge is just the posterior odds.
Why it matters
The closed form is the reference implementation. Every trained D is an approximation of this curve, and the gap between them is the U02 approximation gap wearing a new costume.
Transferable principle
Solve the inner problem in closed form when you can. companion The analytic best response is the yardstick for every learned approximation.

C04 · The JS identity

The game minimizes JS in disguise

Plug D* into V and write m = (p_data + p_g)/2, the midpoint density. From the lesson C04 V = E over p_data of log(p_data/2m) + E over p_g of log(p_g/2m) = KL(p_data || m) + KL(p_g || m) − 2. The sum of two KLs is twice the Jensen-Shannon divergence, so max_D V(G, D) = 2 JS(p_data || p_g) − 2, in bits. At equilibrium p_g = p_data, JS = 0, V = −2.

On the toy: trapezoid integration on a 200001-point grid gives V(G, D*) = −1.3537 and 2 JS − 2 = −1.3537, so JS = 0.3231 bits, identity error 4.4e-16. From the lesson C04 The sweep: mu_g = 0 gives −1.7324, mu_g = 2 gives −0.6314, the identity holding at every point. Note mu_g = 0 does not reach −2: the means match but sig_g = 0.5 differs from 1, so JS stays above 0.

The honest failure: disjoint supports. From the lesson C04 Move mu_g to 10 with sig_g = 0.5. JS saturates at 1 bit, the maximum, no matter how far apart the supports are, and its gradient in the generator parameters is exactly 0. The game gives G no direction. This is the reason the course needs U04 (Wasserstein).

Nugget 04 · C04

The game minimizes JS in disguise.

What happened
A two-player contest over samples turned out to be exactly Jensen-Shannon minimization, verified to 4.4e-16 on the toy.
Why it matters
The disguise is load-bearing: it says what the game optimizes (JS), what equilibrium means (JS = 0), and where the story breaks (disjoint supports, where JS goes flat and blind).
Transferable principle
Reduce the game to the quantity it optimizes. companion Once you know the true objective, its failure modes become predictions, not surprises.

C05 · The non-saturating loss

The gradient dies where help is needed most

G minimizes E over z of log(1 − D(G(z))). Write the logit t with D = sigmoid(t). From the lesson C05 d/dt log(1 − sigmoid(t)) = −sigmoid(t) = −D. Early in training D is confident: it crushes fakes, so D(G(z)) sits near 0, and the gradient sits near 0. G gets almost no signal exactly when it is worst. At t = 0 (D = 0.5): gradient −0.5. At t = −6.907 (D = 0.001): gradient −0.001, a 500x collapse.

The fix: L_G = E over z of −log D(G(z)). From the lesson C05 d/dt [−log sigmoid(t)] = −(1 − D). At D = 0.001 the gradient is −0.999: full strength, pointing G toward higher D. At D = 0.5 both losses agree (−0.5). The fix changes the early dynamics, not the equilibrium.

Bench. Move the logit and watch both gradients. Illustration Exact sigmoid identities from C05.


D = 0.0010 · minimax grad = −0.0010 · non-saturating grad = −0.9990

The deeper identity, at optimal D: From the lesson C05 E over p_g of −log2 D* = KL(p_g || p_data) − KL(p_g || m) + 1, in bits, verified to 2e-16 (both sides 1.7910 on the toy). The minus sign rewards p_g for concentrating where m is small relative to p_g: concentration pressure. This is why the fix helps early and can sharpen samples later.

Nugget 05 · C05

The gradient dies where help is needed most.

What happened
The theoretically correct generator loss gave a 500x weaker gradient (−0.001 vs −0.999) precisely when the generator was worst.
Why it matters
Correct at the optimum is not the same as trainable from the start. The non-saturating loss is a one-line change with no new computation that keeps the signal alive.
Transferable principle
Check the gradient where the model is worst, not where it is best. companion Saturation hides at the start of training, far from the equilibrium analysis.

C06 · Mode collapse

Beauty is not coverage

p_data has two humps: 0.5 N(−2, 1) + 0.5 N(2, 1). The generator learns p_g = N(2, 1): it matches the right hump perfectly and assigns the left hump almost no mass. From the lesson C06 Samples look great (every sample is a plausible right-hump draw) but half the data distribution is absent. Sample quality alone cannot see the missing half. You need a coverage number, not a beauty score.

Why the game allows it: the minimax G gradient only sees the response of D to the samples G actually produces. From the lesson C06 If G never emits left-hump samples, the confident verdict of D at x = −2 never reaches G as a gradient: there is no fake sample there to push. Collapse is not a bug in the code. It is a stable attractor of the dynamics. The measured 0.0232 sits within 2 SE of the predicted 0.0228 (SE = 0.0015): the diagnostic is calibrated.

Nugget 06 · C06

Beauty is not coverage.

What happened
A generator that dropped half the data produced flawless-looking samples. The JS of 0.2809 bits did not sound alarming. The coverage number (0.0232 vs 0.4952) named the missing half.
Why it matters
Every sample-quality metric passes a collapsed generator by construction. Coverage has to be measured separately, with a number that counts the absent.
Transferable principle
Measure what the samples omit, not only what they show. companion A metric that cannot see absence will certify hollow success.

C07 · Gradient pathology

Three pathologies, three different breaks

Beyond saturation, two more things go wrong. From the lesson C07 (1) Saturation (C05): the minimax G gradient goes to 0 when D is confident. Breaks: G stops learning. (2) Explosion: the log-boundary gradients blow up when D hits 0 or 1. At D = 1e-9 on a fake: log2 D = −29.90 bits for one sample, and d/dD log2 D = 1/(D ln 2) = 1.44e9. One confident mistake dominates the batch gradient with a billion-scale spike. (3) Misleading signal: a too-strong D gives gradients that are large but point from a D far from D*. Breaks: the direction, not the size.

The repairs, each partial: From the lesson C07 the non-saturating loss fixes (1) only. Clipping D to [eps, 1 − eps] with eps = 1e-7 tames (2): the spike becomes 1.44e7, still large but bounded and the same bound every step. The clip trades an unbounded spike for a known bias. Nothing in the vanilla game fixes (3): that is what update balance (C11) manages. Note the mirror: at D = 0.999 the non-saturating gradient dies (−0.001) while the minimax holds (−0.999). Each loss has its own dead zone.

C08 · Conditional GAN

Ask for the class you want

The vanilla G maps noise z to x, with no input that says which kind of sample to make. From the lesson C08 Feed the label y to both players: G(z, y) and D(x, y). The objective splits per class: V = E over y of V_y, one minimax game per class, sharing parameters. Conditioning shares every weight across classes. One GAN per class shares nothing and starves rare classes.

On the toy, class 0 is N(−2, 1) and class 1 is N(2, 1). From the lesson C08 The per-class best referees: D*(x = −2, y = 0) = 0.3333, D*(x = 2, y = 1) = 0.3333. The cross term D*(x = −2, y = 1) = 1.0000: a left-hump sample labeled class 1 is obviously real data with a wrong label, so the conditioned D is certain.

The failure case: G drops y. From the lesson C08 Then D(x, y) learns to ignore y too, the pooled metrics look fine, and every class-conditional metric fails. Diagnose per class, always.

C09 · DCGAN

Count the parameters, check the shapes

MLPs on pixels ignore locality: parameters explode and the discriminator memorizes. From the lesson C09 The DCGAN recipe (Radford et al., 2015) is four constraints, each with a reason: strided convolutions instead of pooling (the net learns its own resampling), batch normalization in both nets (keeps activations where gradients flow), no fully connected hidden layers (locality everywhere), ReLU in G with tanh output and LeakyReLU in D (no dead gradients in the critic).

The authored toy generator: z in R^100, fully connected to 4x4x256, then three deconvolutions to (32, 32, 3). From the lesson C09 Exact counts: fc 413696, deconv 256→128: 524416, deconv 128→64: 131136, deconv 64→3: 3075. Total 1072323 parameters. Shapes double each stage: (4,4,256) → (8,8,128) → (16,16,64) → (32,32,3). The count is the check that the architecture is what you think it is.

C10 · Bi-directional GAN

The way back: x to z

A GAN has no encoder: given a real x there is no z with G(z) = x. From the lesson C10 Train an encoder E alongside: E maps x to z. Two joint distributions over pairs (x, z): p_G(x, z) = p_z(z) p_G(x | z) from the generator side, p_E(x, z) = p_data(x) p_E(z | x) from the encoder side. BiGAN (Donahue et al., 2016): the discriminator takes pairs. D(x, z) bets whether the pair came from the encoder side or the generator side. At the optimum the joints match, so the marginals match (still a GAN) and E inverts G in distribution, with no KL term and no reconstruction loss.

The linear toy: G(z) = 2z, E(x) = x/2. From the lesson C10 Cycle error on 5 seed draws: max |z − E(G(z))| = 0.0, max |x − G(E(x))| = 0.0. Exact invertibility, but linear maps invert: a net E and net G need the pair game to couple them. The failure case: E ignores x and outputs prior noise. The game value is unchanged but the encoder is useless. Diagnose with mutual information I(x, E(x)) on held-out data: it must be well above 0.

C11 · Update balance

How many referee steps per painter step

Theory wants max_D: D at its best response before each G move. From the lesson C11 In practice that means k gradient steps on D per G step. Toy arithmetic (authored): D and G have 100000 parameters each, batch 64. One D step costs about 2 × P × B = 12800000 flop-units. k = 5 costs 64000000 per round against 12800000 for k = 1: 5x the D compute per G update.

Both extremes break. From the lesson C11 k too small: D too weak, G chases noise, the value is not the game value. k too large: D near-perfect, and under the minimax loss the G gradient vanishes while under the non-saturating loss G follows an accurate critic that gives no path back. The practical rule: k in 1..5, with the non-saturating loss tolerating larger k. The alternative: two timescales, one step each with lr_D above lr_G (Heusel et al., 2017). The diagnostic bands: D accuracy 0.5 means D learned nothing, 1.0 means G learns nothing (minimax), 0.6 to 0.9 is healthy.

C12 · Sample evaluation

Rank the generators, then distrust the ranking

G1: collapsed N(2, 1) on the two-hump data. G2: the true mixture. From the lesson C12 Both produce plausible samples. Eyeballing says tie. Eyeballing is a quality metric and quality is not coverage.

JS to p_data (bits)0.28090
mean gap2.00630.0119 measured, 0 within 2 SE

Every metric ranks G2 first, and each one says why. From the lesson C12 No single column tells the whole story: JS = 0.2809 does not name the missing mode, the mean gap does not see tail shape, coverage needs a chosen region. The rule: no single metric certifies a generator. Report a divergence-flavored number, a moment number, and a coverage number. Score on held-out data (U01-C12), report the SE, inspect samples by hand as the last gate. The shippable report: number + SE + support verdict + sample sheet.

Nugget 07 · C12

No metric certifies a generator.

What happened
Three metrics ranked G2 over G1 unanimously, yet each one was blind to something: JS did not name the mode, the mean gap did not see tails, coverage needed a hand-picked threshold.
Why it matters
A metric proves the generator passed that metric, nothing more. G1 passes every quality-only metric ever invented. The U02 research-critique trap (JS = 0.001 from a small critic on N = 500) measured the critic, not the generator.
Transferable principle
Triangulate: divergence, moment, coverage. companion One number is a press release. Three numbers from different families are evidence.

Chapter plate

The cost ledger

The lesson closes with a ledger. From the lesson Chapter plate Cost without the ideas: no likelihood means no training signal, and good samples stay a matter of taste. Cost with the ideas: one game (minimax) whose best response is the Bayes ratio, one identity (max_D V = 2 JS − 2) that names what the game minimizes, one loss fix (non-saturating) that keeps gradients alive, one diagnostic family (coverage numbers) that sees what sample quality hides, and three architectures (conditional, DCGAN, BiGAN) that steer, scale, and invert the generator. Tradeoff in one line: the game trades densities and convergence guarantees for sharp samples from samples alone, and every pathology in C05 to C07 and C11 is the price of that trade stated exactly. Connection: the Wasserstein game (U04) replaces the JS story to fix disjoint supports. The VAE (U05) replaces the game with a bound to fix coverage. Diffusion (U07) chains denoising steps to avoid the adversary entirely.
