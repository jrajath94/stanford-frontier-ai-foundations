Unit1

Unit 1

Menu, kitchen, critic

The running toy: x lives in R^2, z in R^1. From the lesson C01-C12 The prior is p(z) = N(0, 1). The encoder outputs mu = 0.4 and sigma^2 = 0.5 for the data point x = [1.2, 0.4]. The decoder is p(x | z) = N(W z + b, s^2 I) with W = [[1.0], [0.5]], b = [0, 0], s = 0.5. The one drawn sample uses eps = 0.6, giving z = 0.824264.

The arc: three parts define the model (C01), Jensen splits the objective into two fighting terms (C02), the Gaussian posterior makes the KL free (C03-C05), the decoder prices reconstruction (C06), the tax collapses the model (C07) until beta reprices it (C08), and then inference, the bound check, and the battery (C09-C12).

Unit2

Unit 2 · Prior, encoder, decoder

Three parts, three jobs

A generative model must do two jobs: assign a number p(x) to data, and produce new x by sampling. From the lesson C01 The prior p(z) = N(0, 1) says which codes are plausible before seeing data. The decoder p(x | z) says how a code becomes data: a Gaussian with mean W z + b and fixed std s = 0.5. The encoder q(z | x) is the reverse direction: given x, which codes could explain it? On the toy it answers mu = 0.4, sigma^2 = 0.5 for x = [1.2, 0.4].

The lesson mental model: the prior is the menu of allowed codes, the decoder is the kitchen that turns a code into a dish, the encoder is the critic that tastes a dish and guesses the recipe code. companion The prior and decoder alone define a generative model, but they give no way to infer z from x and no tractable p(x). The encoder is the inference machine that makes the fit possible.

At the drawn z = 0.824264: From the lesson C01 prior density 0.2844, encoder density 0.4703. The encoder puts more mass near its own mean than the prior does. This mismatch is what the KL term will price. The failure case: the encoder outputs sigma^2 near 0, q becomes a point mass, the model is a plain autoencoder, and the KL term explodes. The toy keeps sigma^2 = 0.5, safely away.

01Nugget 01 · C01
Nugget 01 · C01
The lesson

Three parts, three jobs.

What happened. The model split into prior (which codes are plausible), decoder (codes to data), and encoder (data to codes). The encoder exists because the marginal p(x) has no tractable form without it.

Why it matters. Each part owns a distinct failure: a bad prior misplaces mass, a bad decoder blurs, a collapsed encoder ignores the input. Blaming the wrong part wastes the fix.

Transferable principle. Give every job an owner. A system with three jobs and one component has two jobs nobody is responsible for.

UNIT 02
Unit3

Unit 3 · The ELBO split

Two terms that fight

log p(x) = log integral over z of p(x | z) p(z) dz. From the lesson C02 Multiply and divide by q(z | x), apply Jensen (log is concave): log p(x) ≥ E_q[log p(x | z)] − KL(q(z | x) || p(z)). This is the ELBO. The reconstruction term wants q to put mass on codes that decode well. The KL term wants q close to the prior. They fight: the first wants a sharp, informative q, the second wants a lazy q equal to the prior. The fit is the negotiated settlement.

On the toy with the drawn z = 0.824264: From the lesson C02 reconstruction = −0.7342 nats (−1.0593 bits). KL = 0.1766 nats (0.2547 bits). One-sample ELBO = −0.9108 nats (−1.3140 bits).

The trap: a 1-sample ELBO can exceed the true log p(x). From the lesson C02 On the toy the 1-sample value is −0.9108 nats while log p(x) = −1.9341 nats. The bound holds in expectation (−3.2382 nats, C10), not per sample. Never report a single-sample ELBO as a likelihood.

02Nugget 02 · C02
Nugget 02 · C02
The lesson

The bound holds in expectation.

What happened. The 1-sample ELBO (−0.9108 nats) sat above the true log p(x) (−1.9341 nats), looking like a violated bound. The expected ELBO (−3.2382 nats) sat below it. No violation: the guarantee is on the expectation.

Why it matters. Monte Carlo noise can fake a bound violation or a bound triumph. Any per-sample number needs its expectation beside it before it means anything.

Transferable principle. Check the guarantee at the level it was made. A bound on the mean says nothing about any single draw.

UNIT 03
Unit4

Unit 4 · The Gaussian posterior

The witness with two numbers

The true posterior p(z | x) is unknown and intractable. From the lesson C03 The lesson replaces it with q(z | x) = N(mu(x), sigma^2(x)): mu says where the plausible codes sit, sigma^2 says how uncertain the encoder is. On the toy: mu = 0.4, sigma^2 = 0.5, std 0.7071. The drawn z = 0.824264 sits 0.6 sigma above the mean, consistent with eps = 0.6.

Why Gaussian: three reasons. From the lesson C03 Sampling is trivial. The KL to the standard normal prior has a closed form (C05), so half the ELBO needs no Monte Carlo. Reparameterization works (C04). The price: a Gaussian cannot capture a two-humped true posterior. That price is exactly the bound gap. The lesson parameterizes logvar, not sigma^2: logvar = ln 0.5 = −0.6931, so the network can output any real number and sigma^2 stays positive.

Unit5

Unit 5 · Reparameterization

Freeze the noise, keep the gradient

The reconstruction term is E over z of log p(x | z). Its gradient with respect to mu must pass through the sampling step. From the lesson C04 Reparameterization rewrites z = mu + sigma × eps with eps ~ N(0, 1) fixed, so the gradient of E[f(z)] is E over eps of f'(mu + sigma eps): the randomness is frozen and the derivative is ordinary calculus. The score-function alternative is valid for any q but multiplies the function value by the score, coupling magnitude noise with direction noise.

Measured on f(z) = z^2, z ~ N(0.4, 0.25), 64 samples, seed 0: From the lesson C04 true d/dmu E[f] = 0.8. Reparameterized mean 0.6244, SE 0.1345. Score-function mean 0.5671, SE 0.2622. Both bracket the truth within 3 SE. The score SE is about 2x the reparameterized SE. The wall: discrete latents. There is no differentiable path from a parameter to a coin flip, which is exactly the wall U06 climbs.

eps = 0.6000 · z = 0.4 + 0.7071 × eps = 0.8243 · path gradient of z^2: 2z = 1.6485

03Nugget 03 · C04
Nugget 03 · C04
The lesson

Freeze the noise, keep the gradient.

What happened. Rewriting the draw as mu + sigma × eps moved the randomness out of the parameter path. Gradients flowed through ordinary calculus with half the standard error of the score estimator.

Why it matters. This is why VAEs train with ordinary backprop and no custom gradient code. The estimator choice is load-bearing: the same objective with the score estimator needs far more samples for the same signal.

Transferable principle. Separate the noise from the parameters. When randomness blocks the gradient, re-express it as fixed noise plus a deterministic map.

UNIT 05
Unit6

Unit 6 · The analytical KL

The half of the ELBO that needs no sampling

KL(N(mu, sigma^2) || N(0, 1)) = 0.5 (mu^2 + sigma^2 − 1 − log sigma^2), in nats. From the lesson C05 Every term has a job: mu^2 prices moving the mean off the prior, sigma^2 − 1 prices the wrong spread, and −log sigma^2 explodes as sigma^2 goes to 0, which is what stops the encoder from collapsing to a point mass (when the term is present and weighted enough. C07 shows when it fails anyway).

On the toy: From the lesson C05 mu = 0.4, sigma^2 = 0.5: KL = 0.5 (0.16 + 0.5 − 1 + 0.6931) = 0.1766 nats = 0.2547 bits. Monte Carlo with 200000 samples (seed 0): 0.1760 nats (0.2539 bits). Agreement within 0.005 nats. The formula assumes the prior is exactly N(0, 1): with a learned or mixture prior this closed form is wrong and the KL must be estimated.

Unit7

Unit 7 · The decoder likelihood

The price of the painting

p(x | z) = N(W z + b, s^2 I). From the lesson C06 The decoder promises: x is the decoded mean plus isotropic Gaussian noise with std s. With the drawn z = 0.824264: xhat = [0.824264, 0.412132], squared error 0.141310, reconstruction = −0.7342 nats (−1.0593 bits). With xhat = x the term reduces to the noise floor: −d log s − (d/2) log(2 pi) = −0.4516 nats. That floor is irreducible: even a perfect decoder pays it.

s serves two jobs: the noise scale of the generative story, and the exchange rate between squared error and nats. From the lesson C06 Halve s and the same squared error costs 4x the nats. A learned s can game the objective by shrinking, which is why the toy fixes s = 0.5. The failure case: the decoder ignores z (xhat constant in z). Then the reconstruction term is flat in the encoder and all pressure falls on the KL term, which drives q to the prior. That is the collapse mechanism of C07.

Unit8

Unit 8 · Posterior collapse

The tax on the code

Posterior collapse: q(z | x) = p(z) for all x. The encoder ignores the input and the KL term is 0. From the lesson C07 On the toy: mu = 0, sigma^2 = 1 (KL = 0.0 exactly), decoder outputs xhat = x (squared error 0), reconstruction = −0.4516 nats, ELBO = −0.4516 nats. The healthy model: ELBO = −0.9108 nats. Collapse wins by 0.4592 nats. The objective prefers the model that uses no latent information at all.

ModelRecon (nats)KL (nats)ELBO (nats)

The lesson mental model: the KL term is a tax on using the latent code. companion If the reconstruction gain from the code is smaller than the tax, the rational model evades the tax by ignoring the code. Collapse is tax evasion, and the ELBO is the tax code that permits it. The hard case: a powerful autoregressive decoder reaches near-perfect reconstruction with z constant, and even beta = 1 collapses. That is the known hard case for text VAEs.

04Nugget 04 · C07
Nugget 04 · C07
The lesson

The tax on the code.

What happened. The collapsed model won the ELBO (−0.4516 vs −0.9108 nats) by paying zero KL tax. The objective did exactly what it says.

Why it matters. A higher ELBO can mean a worse generative model. The ELBO is the fit objective, not the business metric: a collapsed model cannot generate diverse samples. Gate releases on posterior-use diagnostics, never on the ELBO alone.

Transferable principle. Distrust any objective that rewards doing less. When the score improves as the model uses less of its capacity, the score measures frugality, not quality.

Unit9

Unit 9 · Beta-VAE

Beta prices the code

The beta-VAE objective: E_q[log p(x | z)] − beta × KL(q || p). From the lesson C08 Beta = 1 is the ELBO. On the toy (recon −0.7342, KL 0.1766 nats): beta = 1 gives −0.9108, 2 gives −1.0874, 4 gives −1.4405, 8 gives −2.1468 nats. In bits: −1.3140, −1.5688, −2.0782, −3.0972. At beta = 4 the KL price is 4 × 0.1766 = 0.7063 nats. Beta does not change what the model can express. It changes what the model bothers to express.

Bench. Set the tax rate and see who wins. Illustration Lesson numbers: healthy recon −0.7342 with KL 0.1766. Collapsed recon −0.4516 with KL 0.


healthy objective = −0.9108 · collapsed objective = −0.4516 · winner: collapsed

The knob has two bad ends. From the lesson C08 Beta very large forces q = prior: the model generates from pure noise and reconstructs nothing. Beta very small makes the KL term decoration and the model a plain autoencoder.

05Nugget 05 · C08
Nugget 05 · C08
The lesson

Beta prices the code.

What happened. Moving beta from 1 to 8 moved the objective from −0.9108 to −2.1468 nats on fixed model numbers. Nothing about the model changed. Only the tax rate did.

Why it matters. Beta is a dial on the tradeoff, not a better bound: beta ≠ 1 is no longer a lower bound on log p(x). The disentangling story (each dim pays its own KL) needs several dims. The toy has one, so it stays untested here.

Transferable principle. Price the tradeoff explicitly. A weighted objective is a policy choice. Name the weight, and name what it is not (a bound).

Unit 10 · Latent traversal

Walk the code, read the decoder

Sweep z from −2 to 2 in five steps, decoding each. From the lesson C09 On the toy: −2 → [−2, −1], −1 → [−1, −0.5], 0 → [0, 0], 1 → [1, 0.5], 2 → [2, 1]. The decoded points march along the line [1, 0.5] z. Adjacent decoded distance: 1.1180 per unit z, constant here because the decoder is linear. A nonlinear decoder would show varying step sizes.

What a good traversal shows: smooth, meaningful change with no jumps. From the lesson C09 Jumps mean the decoder has dead regions. No change means the swept dim is collapsed. The traversal is the cheapest diagnostic for both, and it catches what the ELBO hides.

Unit 11 · The bound check

The independent referee

Compute log p(x) by brute force: integrate p(x | z) p(z) over a fine z grid (160001 points on [−8, 8]). From the lesson C10 Result: log p(x) = −1.9341 nats (−2.7904 bits). The expected ELBO is −3.2382 nats (closed form), the Monte Carlo estimate −3.0650 nats. Both sit below −1.9341 nats. The gap, 1.3040 nats, equals KL(q || p(z | x)): the price of the Gaussian approximation.

Unit 12 · Inference versus fit

One pass to answer, many passes to learn

Inference: a new x arrives, the encoder outputs mu and logvar in one forward pass, done. From the lesson C11 On the toy encoder mu = 0.3 (x0 + x1): x = [1.2, 0.4] → mu = 0.48. Fit: for each batch, sample z, compute the ELBO, backprop, step. The mean 1-sample ELBO over the four toy points is −1.9467 nats (−2.8084 bits).

*The first row uses the lesson mu = 0.4. The encoder head gives 0.48. The audit script uses the head throughout for the mean.

The encoder is amortized inference. From the lesson C11 Classical variational inference would run an optimization per x to fit q. The VAE pays once (fitting the encoder) and then answers each new x in one pass. The price: a new x far from the fit data gets a poor q.

06Nugget 06 · C11
Nugget 06 · C11
The lesson

Amortize once, answer forever.

What happened. The encoder turned per-point variational optimization into one forward pass per new x, at the cost of fit quality on unfamiliar inputs.

Why it matters. Amortization is the VAE's product advantage over per-point methods: inference ships with a latency budget while the fit stays in the lab. The tradeoff is generalization of the encoder itself.

Transferable principle. Pay the optimization cost once when queries repeat. Amortization wins exactly when the same question is asked many times.

The lesson

Test the contract, not the loss.

What happened. Five checks, each guarding one contract item. The collapsed model passed four of five: only the collapse diagnostic caught it.

Why it matters. The loss going down is not evidence the math is right. Each check names what it guards, so a green battery means the machinery holds, not just that a number moved.

Transferable principle. Write the check that would catch the failure you fear most. A test suite without the scary test is a comfort blanket.

Unit 14 · Source block

The classical ancestor

A Gaussian mixture has discrete latents (component assignments) and an exact E-step. From the lesson GMM/EM bridge On the toy (w = [0.6, 0.4], m = [−1, 1], v = 0.25): at x = 0.3, component 2's responsibility is 0.8802 (weighted densities 0.0162 and 0.1198, denominator 0.1360). The M-step recomputes means as responsibility-weighted averages: on the four points [−1.1, −0.9, 0.9, 1.1], the means move from [−1, 1] to [−0.9987, 0.9995] and the log-likelihood rises from −3.8354547 to −3.8354470. Monotone improvement, guaranteed. The guarantee is monotone, not global: EM finds local optima.

EM is the VAE with an exact E-step and no amortization. companion The VAE encoder is an amortized, approximate E-step. The VAE M-step is a gradient step on the ELBO instead of a closed-form maximization. Everything the VAE does, EM does exactly on small discrete-latent models. The VAE exists because EM does not scale to deep continuous latents.

Unit15

Unit 15

The cost ledger

The lesson closes with a ledger. From the lesson Chapter plate Cost without the ideas: the marginal is intractable, gradients cannot pass a sampler, and a higher ELBO can mean a worse model. Cost with the ideas: three parts (prior, encoder, decoder), one split (reconstruction minus KL), one closed-form KL (0.1766 nats on the toy), one gradient trick (reparameterization), one tax diagnosis (collapse wins by 0.4592 nats), one pricing knob (beta), one bound check (gap 1.3040 nats), and five tests. Tradeoff in one line: the VAE trades exact likelihood for a tractable bound plus amortized inference, and the collapse ledger prices the trade exactly. Connection: discrete latents break the reparameterization trick, which is the wall U06 climbs. Diffusion (U07) chains such bounds across noise steps.
