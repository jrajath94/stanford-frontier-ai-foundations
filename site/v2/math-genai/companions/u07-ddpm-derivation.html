Noise in, noise out

Unit2

Unit 2 · The forward Markov chain

The preserved variance

Each step: q(x_t | x_{t-1}) = N(sqrt(alpha_t) x_{t-1}, beta_t), with alpha_t = 1 − beta_t. From the lesson C01 Markov means x_t depends only on x_{t-1}. Forward means data to noise. No learning happens here. The chain is fixed by the schedule.

The sqrt scaling is load-bearing. From the lesson C01 Without it, repeated noise addition would blow up the variance. With it, Var(x_t) = alpha_t Var(x_{t-1}) + beta_t = 1 whenever x_0 has variance 1. On the toy at t = 50: beta_50 = 0.0099495, alpha_50 = 0.9900505, sqrt(alpha_50) = 0.9950127. Given x_49 = 1.99860, the mean of x_50 is 1.98863 and its std is sqrt(0.0099495) = 0.099747. The failure case: forgetting the scaling (x_t = x_{t-1} + noise). Variance grows linearly with t and x_T is not N(0, 1).

The lesson

The preserved variance.

What happened. A sqrt(alpha_t) scaling factor on each step kept the marginal variance at 1 through all 100 steps, so the chain ends near N(0, 1), the prior sampled at generation.

Why it matters. The whole method stands on the endpoint matching the prior. Drop the scaling and x_T is a wide Gaussian that no standard sampler reproduces. One factor carries the endpoint guarantee.

Transferable principle. Guard the endpoint. A process defined by small steps still needs its final state to land where the next stage expects it. Check the last state first.

UNIT 02
Unit3

Unit 3 · The noise schedule

The curriculum

The schedule is the list beta_1 to beta_T. From the lesson C02 Linear here: beta_t = 0.0001 + (t − 1) × (0.02 − 0.0001) / 99. Values: beta_1 = 0.0001, beta_50 = 0.0099495, beta_100 = 0.02. The schedule decides the SNR trajectory, and the SNR trajectory decides what the network learns at each t. High SNR (t small): nearly clean data, fine detail. Low SNR (t large): mostly noise, global structure. The schedule is the curriculum.

SNR_t = alpha_bar_t / (1 − alpha_bar_t). From the lesson C02 On the toy: t = 1 gives 9999.0, t = 50 gives 3.4879, t = 100 gives 0.5712. The signal dies slowly: at t = 50 the SNR is still 3.49. The endpoint is a toy-schedule fact with consequences: alpha_bar_100 = 0.36356, SNR 0.5712. The chain does not reach pure noise. At generation we sample x_T from N(0, 1), but training never showed the model such a noisy x_100 from this x_0. Prior mismatch: the first reverse steps start out-of-distribution.

Unit4

Unit 4 · The cumulative alpha

One number

Unroll two steps and the independent noise terms merge into one Gaussian with variance 1 − alpha_2 alpha_1. From the lesson C03 Induction: q(x_t | x_0) = N(sqrt(alpha_bar_t) x_0, (1 − alpha_bar_t) I), where alpha_bar_t is the cumulative product of the alphas. Alpha_bar_t is the fraction of the original signal surviving at step t. Everything about the forward process at time t (mean scale, noise scale, SNR) is a function of this one number. The T-step chain collapses to a one-line formula.

On the toy: alpha_bar_1 = 0.9999, alpha_bar_50 = 0.77718008, alpha_bar_100 = 0.36356325. From the lesson C03 Signal fraction at t = 50: sqrt(0.77718) = 0.88157. The failure case: numerical underflow of the cumulative product for large T in float32. Use float64 or log-space. The audit uses float64.

Nugget 02 · C03
The lesson

One number.

What happened. A 100-step stochastic chain collapsed into a one-line formula governed by a single cumulative product. Every forward quantity at time t reads off alpha_bar_t.

Why it matters. This identity is the reason DDPM training is affordable: one jump replaces t simulated steps. Without it, every training step would cost T times more.

Transferable principle. Look for the sufficient statistic of the process. A long chain that collapses to one number has a closed form hiding in it. Find the product, the sum, or the integral that absorbs the steps.

Unit5

Unit 5 · Direct noising

The training workhorse

Reparameterize the closed form: x_t = sqrt(alpha_bar_t) x_0 + sqrt(1 − alpha_bar_t) eps, eps from N(0, 1). From the lesson C04 One line, no loop. On the toy at t = 50: sqrt(0.77718008) = 0.8815753, sqrt(0.22281992) = 0.4720356. x_50 = 0.8815753 × 2.0 + 0.4720356 × 0.5 = 1.7631506 + 0.2360178 = 1.9991684 (audit: 1.99917538). Note how little x_50 moved from x_0 = 2.0: the signal dominates at t = 50 (SNR 3.49). At t = 100 with eps = 0: x_100 = sqrt(0.36356) × 2.0 = 1.20596. The noiseless endpoint still shows x_0.

Unit6

Unit 6 · The posterior coefficients

The tractable trick

Bayes: q(x_{t-1} | x_t, x_0) is proportional to q(x_t | x_{t-1}) q(x_{t-1} | x_0). From the lesson C05 Both factors are Gaussian in x_{t-1}. Multiply, complete the square. The result is Gaussian: beta_tilde_t = (1 − alpha_bar_{t-1}) / (1 − alpha_bar_t) × beta_t, mean = [sqrt(alpha_bar_{t-1}) beta_t / (1 − alpha_bar_t)] x_0 + [sqrt(alpha_t) (1 − alpha_bar_{t-1}) / (1 − alpha_bar_t)] x_t. Conditioning on x_0 is the trick that makes the reverse tractable. Without x_0, q(x_{t-1} | x_t) mixes over all possible x_0 and has no closed form. With x_0, it is one Gaussian with known numbers.

At t = 50: beta_tilde_50 = 0.00960075, coefficient on x_0 = 0.03956209, coefficient on x_50 = 0.96013574. From the lesson C05 Posterior mean: 0.03956209 × 2.0 + 0.96013574 × 1.99917538 = 1.99860391. Std: 0.09798340. The coefficients sum to 0.99969783, about 1: the posterior mean is nearly a convex blend of x_0 and x_t. At t = 1 the blend is all x_0. At t = T it is all x_t. Beta_tilde is always below beta_t: the posterior is sharper than one forward step.

The lesson

The tractable trick.

What happened. An intractable reverse distribution became one Gaussian with known numbers the moment x_0 entered the conditioning set. The hard problem was not solved. It was sidestepped.

Why it matters. The network's entire job (C06) is to stand in for the unknown x_0. Every discrete-latent and diffusion method is an answer to a missing variable, and the missing variable here has a name.

Transferable principle. Condition on the thing you do not have, then learn it. When the exact answer needs an unknown, write the formula with the unknown in it first. The learning target appears by itself.

UNIT 06
Unit7

Unit 7 · The reverse Gaussian

The stand-in

x_0 is unknown at sampling time. From the lesson C06 The network predicts the noise eps_hat. From x_t and eps_hat, solve the direct-noising formula for x_0: x0_hat = (x_t − sqrt(1 − alpha_bar_t) eps_hat) / sqrt(alpha_bar_t). Plug x0_hat into the posterior mean. The reverse distribution is p(x_{t-1} | x_t) = N(mu_rev, sigma_t^2). The network never predicts the mean directly. It predicts the noise (or x_0, or v). The mean is algebra around the prediction.

At t = 50, x_50 = 1.99917538, eps_hat = 0.4: x0_hat = (1.99917538 − 0.4720356 × 0.4) / 0.8815753 = 2.05354466. From the lesson C06 mu_rev = 0.03956209 × 2.05354466 + 0.96013574 × 1.99917538 = 2.00072225. The true x_0 was 2.0. The network's 0.1 noise error moved the mean by 0.0007. With eps_hat = eps = 0.5, x0_hat = 2.0 exactly and mu_rev equals the posterior mean 1.99860391.

The lesson

The stand-in.

What happened. The network predicted noise, not the answer. Algebra turned the noise prediction into an x_0 estimate (2.05354466) and then into the reverse mean (2.00072225).

Why it matters. The indirection is why the parameterization choice matters (C08): it changes what the network outputs but not the sampling rule. Predicting the wrong target makes training harder for no reason.

Transferable principle. Predict the convenient quantity, derive the needed one. The network output and the decision variable need not be the same object. Algebra between them is free.

Unit8

Unit 8 · The ELBO decomposition

Not the bound

The ELBO telescopes into: L_T = KL(q(x_T | x_0) || p(x_T)), the prior term. L_{t-1} = KL(q(x_{t-1} | x_t, x_0) || p(x_{t-1} | x_t)) for t = 2 to T, the denoising terms. L_0 = −log p(x_0 | x_1), the decoder term. From the lesson C07 Each term has a job. L_T says the chain must end at the prior. The middle terms say each reverse step must match the true posterior. L_0 says the last step must hit the data.

On the toy: L_T = 0.7713 nats (1.1127 bits). From the lesson C07 q(x_T | x_0) = N(1.20596, 0.63644), and the KL to N(0, 1) is 0.5 × (1.20596² + 0.63644 − 1 − ln 0.63644) = 0.7713. L_49 = 0.0002337 nats: the two Gaussians share variance beta_tilde_50, so the KL is 0.5 × (1.99860391 − 2.00072225)² / 0.00960075. L_0 = 0.0050005 nats. L_T dominates the toy ELBO: the schedule's noisy endpoint is expensive. Each middle KL is a squared mean difference, because the two Gaussians share a variance.

The lesson

Not the bound.

What happened. The training loss dropped the ELBO's KL coefficients and sampled t uniformly. The result trains better samples but is no longer the bound that was derived.

Why it matters. DDPM with the full ELBO and DDPM with the simplified loss are different models with different likelihoods. The objective optimized is not the bound derived. Reporting the simplified sum as the ELBO mislabels the model.

Transferable principle. Name the objective you actually optimized. A derived bound and a shipped loss can diverge for good reasons. The divergence itself is a fact to report, not a detail to hide.

UNIT 08
Unit9

Unit 9 · Epsilon, x0, and v prediction

Error scaling

From x_t = sqrt(alpha_bar_t) x_0 + sqrt(1 − alpha_bar_t) eps, any one of (x_0, eps) determines the other given x_t. From the lesson C08 Epsilon prediction: the network outputs eps_hat, and x0_hat follows by algebra. x0 prediction: the network outputs x0_hat directly. v prediction: the network outputs v = sqrt(alpha_bar_t) eps − sqrt(1 − alpha_bar_t) x_0, a blend, and both follow by rotation. The three are algebraically equivalent given a perfect network, but they weight errors differently across t. Epsilon prediction makes the loss scale-free in x_0: at high t the target eps is N(0, 1) regardless of the data scale. x0 prediction at high t asks the network to recover a faint signal from heavy noise: a harder target.

On the toy at t = 50: eps = 0.5, eps_hat = 0.4, error 0.1. From the lesson C08 Equivalent x0 error: 2.05354466 − 2.0 = 0.05354466. The 0.1 noise error becomes a 0.0535 signal error through the 1/sqrt(alpha_bar_t) = 1.1343 scaling and the sqrt(1 − alpha_bar_t) = 0.4720 factor: 0.1 × 0.4720 / 0.8816 = 0.0535. The round trip eps → x0_hat → eps_hat returns 0.4 exactly. The failure case: mixing parameterizations between training and sampling. The sampling algebra must invert the same transform the network was trained on.

The lesson

Error scaling.

What happened. A 0.1 error in noise became a 0.0535 error in signal through the schedule's scaling factors. The same mistake has different sizes in different parameterizations.

Why it matters. The parameterization is a preconditioner on the learning problem. Epsilon prediction keeps the target N(0, 1) at every t. The wrong target makes some timesteps artificially hard.

Transferable principle. Choose the target the network can hit. Equivalent formulations are not equally learnable. Normalize the target so the error means the same thing everywhere.

Unit 10 · Timestep sampling

Equal effort everywhere

t is uniform over {1, ..., T}, independently per example. From the lesson C09 On the toy with 1000 draws (seed 0): counts per t range from 3 to 16, mean exactly 10.0. Uniform t is a choice to spend equal training effort at every noise level. The ELBO's KL coefficients would spend effort proportional to their weights. The simplified loss abandons that weighting, and uniform sampling implements the abandonment. The spread 3 to 16 is ordinary multinomial noise: the std of each count is sqrt(1000 × 0.01 × 0.99) = 3.13, so the range sits within about 2.2 sigma.

Unit11

Unit 11 · The training loss

One step, one squared error

Sample x_0 from the data. Sample t uniform from {1, ..., T}. Sample eps from N(0, 1). Form x_t by direct noising. Ask the network for eps_hat(x_t, t). Loss: ||eps − eps_hat||². From the lesson C10 On the five toy pairs: (0.5, 0.4) gives 0.01, (−0.2, 0.1) gives 0.09, (0.7, 0.65) gives 0.0025, (−0.9, −0.7) gives 0.04, (0.1, 0.0) gives 0.01. Mean: 0.0305. A perfect network scores 0. One network evaluation per example. The t sampling and the noising are O(1).

The lesson hinge: the loss takes the denoising-score-matching form in disguise. From the lesson C10 The network learns to point from x_t back toward x_0 at every noise level. The lesson notes the bridge to score-based models and marks it as U09 material.

Unit12

Unit 12 · The boundary variance

Second-order choice

The ELBO leaves sigma_t² underdetermined. From the lesson C11 Beta_t and beta_tilde_t are both defensible (upper and lower bounds), and sigma_t = 0 is a third choice. On the toy at t = 50 with z = 0.3: x_49 = 2.03064640 (upper), 2.03011727 (lower), 2.00072225 (zero). The variance choice barely moves one step (the two nonzero values differ by 4 percent) but compounds over 100 steps. It is a boundary condition of the theory: the ELBO is flat in this direction, so the choice is empirical. This is also why a different sampling rule can reuse the same trained network without retraining.

The failure case: treating the variance as the important choice while the mean formula is wrong. From the lesson C11 Variance is second-order. The mean is first-order.

Unit13

Unit 13 · The sampling loop

The loop is the product

Sample x_T from N(0, 1). For t = T down to 1: predict eps_hat, form mu_rev, sample x_{t-1} from N(mu_rev, sigma_t²). At t = 1, output the mean (no noise). From the lesson C12 On the toy with x_100 = 1.7, eps_hat = 0.1 each step, z draws [0.3, −0.2, 0.5], sigma from beta_tilde: x_99 = 1.75690526, x_98 = 1.74406522, x_97 = 1.82846911. Training never runs this loop. Evaluation lives or dies by it. Every step's error compounds: a biased eps_hat drifts the trajectory. The three toy steps show the mechanism, not a real sample (the network here is a constant 0.1).

Cost: T network evaluations per sample. From the lesson C12 The sampling loop is the deployed artifact, and the loop cost, not the training loss, sets the serving budget. The failure case: reusing one z draw seed across samples. Correlated outputs. Fresh noise per step per sample.

The lesson

The loop is the product.

What happened. Three hand steps turned x_100 = 1.7 into x_97 = 1.82846911 through 3 of the 100 network evaluations a real sample needs. Training never ran the loop. Serving is the loop.

Why it matters. The training loss does not set the serving budget. The loop does. Any sampling-cost attack (fewer steps, same network) changes the economics without retraining.

Transferable principle. Price the serving path, not the training path. The artifact the user meets is the inference loop. Optimize and budget for that loop first.

The cost ledger

The lesson closes with a ledger. From the lesson Chapter plate Cost without the ideas: noise added all at once breaks the Gaussian posterior, the reverse has no closed form without x_0, and the loop costs T evaluations nobody budgeted.
