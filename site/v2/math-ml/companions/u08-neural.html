Section 2 · C02

Backprop: the chain rule walks backward

From the lessonC02

Computed numbers, 2026-10-06. do = o - y = -1.7. dw2 = do·a = -1.36. db2 = do = -1.7. da = do·w2 = -2.55. dz = da(z greater than 0) = -2.55. dw1 = dz·x = -5.1. db1 = dz = -2.55. One forward pass storing z, then the backward walk above. Six lines.

From the lessonC02

The check: central finite differences with h = 1e-7 give dw1 = -5.1000000012 and dw2 = -1.3600000015, matching the analytic values to 8 digits. U03's chain rule, running in reverse through the layers.

Nugget 01 · Backprop is the chain rule with memory

dw1 = -5.1 analytically, -5.1000000012 by finite difference. The backward pass reuses every intermediate it stored.

What happened
The lesson walks the error backward through two layers, multiplying local slopes, and audits against finite differences to 8 digits.
Why it matters
Storing z during the forward pass is what makes the backward pass cheap. Memory buys the gradient.
Transferable principle
companion When a computation needs its own history, store the intermediates. Recomputation is the tax on forgetting.

Section 3 · C03

Convolution: a difference detector

From the lessonC03

The kernel [1, 0, -1] is a difference detector. The constant slope gives a constant response. Both np.convolve and the explicit loop give [-2, -2]. Computed 2026-10-06. A note on convention: deep learning uses cross-correlation, no kernel flip. With learned kernels the flip is irrelevant. With hand kernels the sign convention matters and must be stated.

Section 4 · C04

Locality and weight sharing: 102,500 vs 208

From the lessonC04

Computed numbers, 2026-10-06. Dense count 102,500. Conv count for eight 5x5 filters: 5·5·8 + 8 = 208. Ratio about 493 to 1. Sharing ties the weights across positions, so the count is per filter, not per position. The assumption is translation invariance: an edge is an edge wherever it sits. The +100 and +8 are the biases, one per output.

Nugget 02 · Sharing is a bet on symmetry

102,500 parameters become 208. The 493x discount is purchased with one assumption: position does not matter.

What happened
The lesson counts both: dense 102,500, eight 5x5 filters 208, and names translation invariance as the funding assumption.
Why it matters
Every symmetry you can name is parameters you do not pay for. CNNs are dense nets with a symmetry budget.
Transferable principle
companion Before adding parameters, ask what symmetry the data honors. Share across it.

From the lessonC05

Section 6 · C06

Recurrence: the same cell, every step

From the lessonC06

Section 7 · C07

Gradient decay: ten multiplications by 0.5

From the lessonC07

dh_10/dh_0 is the product over t = 1..10 of dh_t/dh_{t-1} = w^10. Each tanh derivative at most 1 can only shrink it further. The check is the exponent: 10·log10(w). With w = 0.5: 0.5^10 = 9.7656e-4, log10 = -3.01. Ten steps, three orders of magnitude gone. With w = 1.5 the product explodes instead. In the matrix case the spectral radius plays the role of |w|.

From the lessonC07 Decay bench: pick w and steps. The widget computes w^steps, the lesson's gradient factor.

Lesson endpoints: 0.5^10 = 9.7656e-4. Try 1.5^10 for the explosion.

Nugget 03 · The exponent is the whole story

0.5^10 = 9.7656e-4. Ten multiplications erased three orders of magnitude. The gradient never stood a chance.

What happened
The lesson writes the 10-step gradient as w^10 and evaluates both sides of 1: decay at 0.5, explosion at 1.5.
Why it matters
Vanishing and exploding gradients are arithmetic, not mystery. Long sequences multiply the problem by its length.
Transferable principle
companion When a signal crosses many multiplications, check the base against 1. The exponent does the rest.

Section 8 · C08

Gates: the constant error carousel

From the lessonC08

Computed numbers, 2026-10-06. c_new = 0.4098, term by term 0.1832 + 0.2266. h_new = o·tanh(c_new) = 0.6502(0.3885) = 0.2525. The cell update is additive: c_new = f·c + i·g. When f = 1 and i = 0, the cell copies itself exactly and the gradient flows unchanged. That is the constant error carousel. Each gate sits in (0, 1). h_new sits in (-1, 1).

Nugget 04 · Addition is the memory trick

c_new = f·c + i·g. With f = 1 and i = 0, the cell copies itself and the gradient flows unchanged across time.

What happened
The lesson runs the six gate lines in order and shows the additive update preserving the cell exactly at the carousel setting.
Why it matters
Multiplication decays. Addition preserves. The LSTM's memory is an architectural choice, not a training miracle.
Transferable principle
companion To carry information across many steps, add, do not multiply. Residuals are the same idea wearing a new name.

From the lessonC09

Outputs: row 0 = [3.0, 4.0], row 1 = [3.3019, 4.3019]. Softmax with max subtraction is exact: subtract the row max before exp. The derivation, then the code: S = QKᵀ/sqrt(d), shift, exp, normalize.

Section 10 · C10

The transformer: shapes design the block

From the lessonC10

Per-head dim = d/H = 8/2 = 4, must divide evenly. FFN: (5, 8) to (5, 16) to (5, 8). Residual add again: (5, 8). The residual X + sublayer(X) needs identical shapes. That constraint designs the whole block. Scores (5,5): each of 5 queries attends to 5 keys. The two residual adds both see (5, 8).

Nugget 05 · Shapes are the architecture

8/2 = 4 must divide evenly. (5, 8) must meet (5, 8) at every residual add. The block is a shape contract with attention inside.

What happened
The lesson traces every intermediate shape and shows the residual constraint forcing the design.
Why it matters
Most transformer bugs are shape bugs. The architecture is arithmetic with a contract.
Transferable principle
companion When a block confuses you, trace the shapes. The contract explains the choices.

Section 11 · C11

Normalization: mean 0, variance 1, by construction

From the lessonC11

Batch norm: statistics over the batch axis, per feature. Derivation: mean(x) = 0 and var(x) = 1 after the transform, by construction. Gamma and beta then reintroduce the scale the next layer wants. Normalize first, then let the layer choose its own scale.

Section 12 · C12

Optimization: Adam's first step is a signed step

From the lessonC12

Computed numbers, 2026-10-06. Gradient g, eps: SGD update = -eta·g = [-0.005, 0.003]. Derivation: at step 1 the bias correction divides out the (1 - beta) factors exactly, so m_hat/sqrt(v_hat) = sign(g) per coordinate. Adam's first step is a signed step of size eta. The adaptive method starts democratic: every coordinate moves by eta, direction by sign.

Nugget 06 · Adam starts by ignoring magnitudes

Step 1: m_hat/sqrt(v_hat) = sign(g). Every coordinate moves eta in its sign direction. Magnitude joins later.

What happened
The lesson derives the bias-corrected first step and shows it reduces to a signed step of size eta.
Why it matters
Early Adam steps ignore gradient scale at first. That is why it trains out of the box where SGD needs tuning.
Transferable principle
companion When gradients vary wildly in scale, sign-based steps survive. Normalize the direction before you trust the size.

Nugget 07 · The unit in one line

Compose affine maps and nonlinearities, share weights across space and time, guard the gradient's magnitude, and let attention choose the context.

What happened

Why it matters

Transferable principle
companion Learn each block's arithmetic cold. Architecture papers are compositions of these moves.
