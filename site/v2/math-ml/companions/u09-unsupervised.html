Nugget 01 · K-means descends, but the start writes the itinerary

J: 0.1700 to 0.133333 on a good start, 145.17 to 0.133333 on a bad one. Monotone, yet start-dependent.

What happened
The lesson runs Lloyd's algorithm from two starts: both land at J = 0.133333 on this toy, via very different paths.
Why it matters
On real data the destinations differ too. One k-means run is a sample. Restarts are the estimate.
Transferable principle
companion When the objective is non-convex, never trust a single run. Restart and keep the best.

Section 2 · C02

Distance and scaling: the ruler decides the neighbor

From the lessonC02

Computed example, 2026-10-06. Raw: nearest of A is B (index 1). After scaling y by 5: distances to B = 10.05, to C = 5.46. Nearest flips to C (index 2). After standardization: nearest returns to B (index 1). The one changed factor is the y scale. Distance is not a fact about the points. It is a fact about the ruler.

Nugget 02 · Distance is a choice, not a fact

Scale y by 5 and the nearest neighbor flips from B to C. Standardize and it flips back. The points never moved.

What happened
The lesson changes only the y scale and watches the nearest-neighbor verdict reverse, then restore under standardization.
Why it matters
K-means, k-NN, and RBF kernels all read distances. Unscaled features let the largest unit win every vote.
Transferable principle
companion Before any distance-based method, standardize. The ruler is part of the model.

Section 4 · C04

Reconstruction: the error is the dropped energy

From the lessonC04

Computed example, 2026-10-06. Reconstruction MSE 0.009172. Dropped eigenvalue 0.018344 / d (d = 2) = 0.009172. Match to 1e-12. The identity is the invariant check: the error of dropping a direction equals that direction's share of the variance. U02's SVD lesson names the price. This is the receipt.

Nugget 03 · Dropping a direction costs exactly its energy

MSE 0.009172 equals 0.018344 / 2 to 1e-12. The reconstruction error is not estimated. It is known.

What happened
The lesson reconstructs on PC1 alone and verifies the error matches the dropped eigenvalue over dimension count.
Why it matters
Compression budgets are exact. You know the price before you pay it.
Transferable principle
companion When you truncate, compute the dropped energy first. The bill arrives with the decision, not after.

Section 5 · C09

The factor bridge: PCA spends on noise too

From the lessonC09

Computed example, 2026-10-06. PCA k = 1 reconstruction MSE on all 3 dims: 1.278512. Noise floor mean (0.01 + 0.01 + 9)/3 = 3.006667. PCA eigenvalues: 7.330603, 3.826997, 0.008538. Note the second eigenvalue 3.83: PCA spends a whole direction on the noisy sensor. A factor model with per-dimension noise would spend its capacity on the signal direction instead.

From the lessonC09

PCA assumes equal noise everywhere. When one sensor is far noisier, that assumption misspends a direction. The factor view prices noise per dimension.

Nugget 04 · Know what your method assumes about noise

Second eigenvalue 3.83: a whole PCA direction spent on the noisy sensor. The method did its job. The assumption was wrong.

What happened
The lesson shows PCA allocating a direction to sensor noise because its equal-noise assumption cannot price dimensions differently.
Why it matters
Method choice is assumption choice. The wrong noise model spends capacity on garbage.
Transferable principle
companion Before you run PCA, ask whether the noise is equal across features. If not, say so and pick accordingly.

Section 6 · C12

Held-out evaluation: the elbow, not a U-turn

From the lessonC12

Computed example, 2026-10-06. Train inertia: 7.6417, 0.3698, 0.3161, 0.1789. Held-out inertia: 14.1768, 0.4439, 0.3360, 0.2939. The drop from k = 1 to k = 2 is 13.73 on held-out, while from k = 2 to k = 5 only 0.15 more. Elbow at k = 2. Honest note: held-out keeps falling slowly past 2 on this easy data. The elbow, not a U-turn, is the verdict.

Nugget 05 · Train inertia always falls. Held-out elbows

Held-out: 14.1768 to 0.4439 to 0.3360 to 0.2939. The 13.73 drop picks k = 2. The rest is noise.

What happened
The lesson scores k on held-out data: a cliff from 1 to 2, then a slow drift. The elbow is the verdict.
Why it matters
Unsupervised models need validation too. Train metrics cannot choose k. They only fall.
Transferable principle
companion Validate the unsupervised choice on held-out data. The elbow is honest. The train curve is flattery.

Nugget 06 · The unit in one line

Cluster by distance on standardized features, keep the high-variance directions, price the dropped energy, and validate k on held-out data.

What happened
Six sections run from Lloyd's iterations to the held-out elbow, each with measured numbers.
Why it matters
Unsupervised learning is assumption management: the ruler, the noise model, and the k.
Transferable principle
companion State the three assumptions before you cluster: the distance, the noise, the k. Then measure.
