Unit 1 · C01

The latent generative process: sum over what you cannot see

From the lessonC01

p(x) = sum over z of p(x, z). The marginal at x = 0: 0.5·Phi(1) + 0.5·Phi(-1) = 0.5. The empirical CDF from ten samples at 0: 3/10 = 0.3. The gap of 0.2 is honest small-n noise, not a bug. Computed 2026-10-06. The latent variable is summed out. The data never shows it.

The lesson

z in {0, 1} with p(z) = 0.5 each. Rename z=0 to z=1 and z=1 to z=0: the x distribution is identical. Every observable is identical. The latent labels are unidentifiable from x alone.

What happened. The lesson draws ten samples, then swaps the names. The marginal at x = 0 stays 0.5. The empirical CDF stays 0.3. Nothing moves.

Why it matters. Any claim about component 0 needs an anchor outside the data. Without one, the name is a costume.

Transferable principle. When a model names something unobserved, ask what anchors the name. No anchor, no meaning.

Unit 2 · C02

The VAE: a bound you can optimize

From the lessonC02

Computed numbers, 2026-10-06. KL = 0.5(mu² + sigma² - 1 - log sigma²) = 0.5(0.16 + 0.25 - 1 + 1.3863) = 0.3981. One sample z = 0.55: log p(x|z) = -0.5(0.7-0.55)² - 0.5 log(2 pi) = -0.9302. ELBO for one sample = -1.3283. The reparameterization trick z = mu + sigma·eps, eps from N(0,1), lets gradients flow through mu and sigma.

From the lessonC02

The check: KL ≥ 0, here 0.3981. The ELBO is a lower bound on log p(x): optimize the bound, lift the likelihood.

The lesson

ELBO = -1.3283 = -0.9302 - 0.3981. Reconstruction pays, the KL term charges, and the sum lower-bounds the truth.

What happened. The lesson computes the one-sample ELBO term by term: log-likelihood -0.9302, KL 0.3981, bound -1.3283.

Why it matters. Intractable likelihoods become tractable bounds. The VAE trains on the bound and hopes the gap stays small.

Transferable principle. When the objective is intractable, bound it and optimize the bound. Then audit the gap.

Unit 3 · C03

The GAN: two players, one value

From the lessonC03

Computed numbers, 2026-10-06. V at start: log 0.8 + log 0.7 = -0.5798. G improves until D(fake) = 0.5: V = log 0.8 + log 0.5 = -0.9163. Lower V is better for G. Train by alternating: one D step up the gradient of V, one G step down the gradient of log(1 - D(G(z))).

From the lessonC03

The check: at D(fake) = 1 the generator term log(0) is negative infinity. The loss saturates in code. The theory needs the limit.

-0.9163Nugget 08 · Lower V is better for G
Nugget 08 · Lower V is better for G
The lesson

V at start: log 0.8 + log 0.7 = -0.5798. G improves until D(fake) = 0.5: V = log 0.8 + log 0.5 = -0.9163. Lower V is better for G. At D(fake) = 1 the generator term is log(0), and the loss must be clipped.

What happened. The lesson scores the game at two stages. The value falls as the generator improves. It then names the saturation: D(fake) = 1 breaks the log.

Why it matters. The game has a scoreboard. The falling V is the training signal. The clip is the guardrail.

Transferable principle. In any adversarial setup, watch the score and the saturation. A score that cannot move is not a signal.

Unit 4 · C04

Objective differences: how far apart, symmetrically

From the lessonC04

Computed numbers, 2026-10-06. JSD = 0.0462 nats. Small: the two toy distributions are close. The checks: JSD ≥ 0 with equality only at p = q, symmetry JSD(p,q) = JSD(q,p), and the log-2 ceiling: log 2 = 0.6931 nats holds. Unlike KL, JSD is symmetric and bounded.

0.0462Nugget 09 · A ruler with a ceiling
Nugget 09 · A ruler with a ceiling
The lesson

JSD = 0.0462 nats on the toy. The ceiling is log 2 = 0.6931 nats. JSD is symmetric and lives in [0, log 2]. Small means the two distributions are close.

What happened. The lesson computes JSD on p = [0.7, 0.3] and q = [0.4, 0.6], then checks symmetry JSD(p,q) = JSD(q,p) and the log-2 ceiling.

Why it matters. Symmetry and a ceiling make JSD a ruler, not just a direction. A divergence with a known maximum reads at a glance.

Transferable principle. Prefer measures with known ceilings when you compare. A bounded number is a readable number.

Unit 5 · C05

Samples versus densities: the staircase and the curve

From the lessonC05

At x = 0: F_10 = 0.3, F = 0.5. The empirical staircase jumps 0.1 per sample. The honest note: with n = 10 the valley between the modes is invisible. No sample fell near x = 0 from the gap side. Ten samples cannot see the valley. The density knows it is there.

2Nugget 02 · Ten samples cannot see a valley
Nugget 02 · Ten samples cannot see a valley
The lesson

F_10 = 0.3 vs F = 0.5 at x = 0. The staircase misses the valley because no sample landed in it.

What happened. The lesson compares the empirical CDF from 10 samples against the true mixture CDF and finds the gap invisible.

Why it matters. Samples and densities answer different questions. A sample is an existence proof. A density is a map.

Transferable principle. When n is small, distrust the sample's silences. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

Unit 6 · C06

The beta sweep: who wins when the KL price rises

From the lessonC06

Computed beta sweep, 2026-10-06. beta = 0: total -0.9302, a plain autoencoder with no KL price. beta = 1: -1.3283, the VAE. beta = 10: -0.9302 + 10(0.3981) = -5.0713. The KL term dominates at beta = 10, and the optimizer prefers the collapsed q (KL 0) with total -1.6695 over the informative q at -5.0713. Posterior collapse, in arithmetic.

From the lessonC06

Adam changes the update, not the loss. The sweep is one line per beta. The lesson is that the objective decides the optimum. Beta prices the KL term.

From the lessonC06 Beta bench: total = -0.9302 + beta × 0.3981. Move beta and watch the KL price.

Lesson endpoints: beta 0 gives -0.9302, beta 1 gives -1.3283, beta 10 gives -5.0713.

3Nugget 03 · Price the KL too high and the model goes silent
Nugget 03 · Price the KL too high and the model goes silent
The lesson

Beta 10: informative q totals -5.0713, collapsed q totals -1.6695. The optimizer picks silence. That is posterior collapse.

What happened. The lesson sweeps beta and shows the KL price crossing the point where ignoring the latent beats using it.

Why it matters. VAEs go silent not from bad training but from bad pricing. The objective, not the optimizer, is the culprit.

Transferable principle. When a model ignores its inputs, check the price of using them. Collapse is economics, not mystery.

Proof assumptions: state the regime with the kill

From the lessonC07

Every theorem in U01 through U10 carries conditions, and the lesson demands they be stated with the claim. A counterexample outside the claim's conditions proves nothing. The regime is part of the result: with n = 10⁴ the curve would be monotone, so the small-n rise needs its six-validation-points qualifier.

0.1438Nugget 10 · Four assumptions wear the proof
Nugget 10 · Four assumptions wear the proof
The lesson

The ELBO proof needs four load-bearing assumptions: q is a valid distribution, the expectation is over q, support matches where q is positive, and log is concave. The toy gap is 0.1438, exactly KL(q || posterior).

What happened. The lesson lists each assumption, then computes the gap two ways. Via the bound: -0.7340 - (-0.8779) = 0.1439 at the reported precision. Via the KL: 0.5·log(0.5/0.25) + 0.5·log(0.5/0.75) = 0.1438.

Why it matters. The gap is exactly the KL to the true posterior. Break an assumption and the gap stops meaning that.

Transferable principle. Audit the assumptions before citing the theorem. Four checks beat one citation.

Unit 8 · C08

Toy counterexamples: the gap is a KL

From the lessonC08

Computed numbers, 2026-10-06. p(x=1) = 0.6(0.2) + 0.4(0.9) = 0.48. log p(x) = -0.7340. Joint: p(x=1, z=0) = 0.12, p(x=1, z=1) = 0.36. ELBO = 0.5 log 0.12 + 0.5 log 0.36 + log 2 = -0.8779. Gap: -0.7340 - (-0.8779) = 0.1438 = KL(q||posterior) ≥ 0. The bound's gap is exactly the posterior mismatch. Each counterexample reuses computed numbers from this build. No fresh toys were invented for the occasion.

4Nugget 04 · The ELBO gap has a name
Nugget 04 · The ELBO gap has a name
The lesson

Gap 0.1438 = KL(q||posterior). The bound is loose exactly where your approximate posterior is wrong.

What happened. The lesson computes the ELBO gap on a discrete toy and identifies it as the KL to the true posterior.

Why it matters. Improving q tightens the bound. The gap is the diagnostic for the approximation, not a fudge factor.

Transferable principle. When a bound disappoints, measure its gap. The gap names the next thing to fix.
