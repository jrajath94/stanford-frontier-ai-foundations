The probabilistic view: noise has a variance

From the lessonC02

sigma²_hat = RSS / n = 1.8 / 4 = 0.45. Max log-likelihood -4.0787. The predictive variance at x = 5 is sigma²(1 + x_*ᵀ(XᵀX)⁻¹x_*) = 1.665. The 1 is the new noise. The second term is the parameter doubt, growing with distance from the data. Computed 2026-10-06.

From the lessonC02

The lesson's moral: least squares is maximum likelihood under Gaussian noise. The same w_hat, a new story about uncertainty.

1.665Nugget 08 · The variance splits in two
Nugget 08 · The variance splits in two
The lesson

Predictive variance at x = 5 is 1.665. It equals 0.45 times (1 + 2.7). The 1 is new noise. The 2.7 is parameter doubt, and it grows with distance from the data.

What happened. The lesson computes sigma²_hat = 0.45 from RSS 1.8 over 4 points. It evaluates x_*ᵀ(XᵀX)⁻¹x_* = 2.7 by hand: 0.7 - 1.5 - 1.5 + 5.0. Then 0.45 · 3.7 = 1.665.

Why it matters. Uncertainty has two sources. New noise never shrinks. Parameter doubt shrinks with data. The split tells you which part more data can fix.

Transferable principle. When a forecast gives a band, split it. Ask which part data can shrink and which part it cannot.

Kernel feature maps: the dot product that missed

From the lessonC06

phi(2)·phi(3) = 43, not 49. The naive map [1, x, x²] misses a cross term. The corrected map phi2(x) = [1, sqrt(2)x, x²] gives 1 + 2·6 + 36 = 49. Match. Computed 2026-10-06. The kernel (1 + 2·3)² = 49 computes the corrected dot product without ever building the map.

The lesson

phi(2)·phi(3) = 43 misses. (1 + 2·3)² = 49 hits. The kernel computes the right dot product in the original coordinates.

What happened. The lesson shows the naive quadratic map undercounts the cross term, fixes it with the sqrt(2) scaling, and notes the kernel shortcut.

Gram matrices: PSD or the kernel is invalid

From the lessonC07

K = [[1, 0.606531, 0.135335], [0.606531, 1, 0.082085], [0.135335, 0.082085, 1]]. Eigenvalues 0.391064, 0.965601, 1.643335. All positive. PSD confirmed. Computed 2026-10-06. One claim: the matrix is PSD. A kernel whose Gram matrix is not PSD is not a kernel.

3.227e6Nugget 10 · PSD is the ticket, not the exam
Nugget 10 · PSD is the ticket, not the exam
The lesson

The Gram eigenvalues are 0.391064, 0.965601, 1.643335. All positive. PSD confirmed. Then the failure case: an RBF Gram with gamma = 1e-6 has condition number 3.227e6. Its alphas sit near one million in magnitude and cancel to fit b. The solve residual reads 4.1e-11.

What happened. The lesson passes the PSD test, then breaks the solve anyway. The eigenvalues certify the geometry. The condition number condemns the arithmetic.

Why it matters. PSD admits the kernel. Conditioning decides whether the computer can use it. Read the condition number alongside the residual.

Transferable principle. A test that passes is not a test that suffices. After the admission test, run the arithmetic test.

Unit 9 · C09

The SVM dual: symmetry solves it

From the lessonC09

By symmetry a+ = a- = a. Dual: 2a - 0.5·4·a²·2 = 2a - 4a². Derivative 2 - 8a = 0 gives a* = 0.25. Dual value 0.25. w = 0.25(1,1) + 0.25(1,1) = [0.5, 0.5]. Primal 0.5||w||² = 0.25. Strong duality holds: the two values agree. Complementary slackness: y_i(w·x_i) = 1 for both, consistent with alpha greater than 0. Computed 2026-10-06.

1 numberNugget 04 · Strong duality is a receipt
Nugget 04 · Strong duality is a receipt
The lesson

Primal 0.25, dual 0.25. Two different optimizations, one number. Agreement certifies both.

What happened. The lesson solves the symmetric dual in closed form: a* = 0.25, w = [0.5, 0.5], and both objectives read 0.25.

Why it matters. Duality gaps measure suboptimality. A zero gap is a proof, not a hope.

Transferable principle. When two formulations must agree, check the gap. It is the cheapest certificate in optimization.

Subgradient steps: shrink toward the violator

From the lessonC10

Subgradient at the noisy point: -y·x = [0.2, 0.2]. Step eta = 0.5: w_new = [1, 1] - 0.5[0.2, 0.2] = [0.9, 0.9]. The step shrinks w toward the violator's side. Computed 2026-10-06. Hinge loss is not smooth at the kink. The subgradient steps anyway.

The lesson

Subgradient [0.2, 0.2] at the noisy point. Step eta = 0.5 moves w from [1, 1] to [0.9, 0.9]. The failure case warns that eta = 5 jumps across the kink and back forever. The fix is eta = 1/t.

What happened. The lesson steps through the hinge kink with eta = 0.5 and lands cleanly. It then names the step size that oscillates: eta = 5.

Why it matters. Nonsmooth objectives punish fixed step sizes. The direction was correct in both cases. The schedule made the difference.

Transferable principle. When an optimizer chatters at a kink, suspect the step size before the model.
