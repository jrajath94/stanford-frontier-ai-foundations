Unit 1 · SL-01 · C01

Finite horizon: no discount, non-stationary policy

From the lessonSL-01 §Source anchor, §Non-stationary policies, §Backward recursion

Chapter 19 used an infinite horizon with discount γ. Here the horizon is finite: the tuple is (S, A, P_sa, T, R) with T > 0, for example T = 100. The payoff is the finite sum R(s₀, a₀) + ... + R(s_T, a_T). No discount factor. γ existed to keep the infinite sum finite. A finite sum needs no discount. The optimal policy now depends on time: π^(t): S → A. The notes' example: a grid with a +1 goal and a +10 goal. Early on, aim for +10. If the dynamics push you near +1 with few steps left, switch to +1. The time left is part of the decision. Solve backward: V*_T(s) = max_a R^(T)(s, a) (20.1), then V*_t(s) = max_a [R^(t)(s, a) + E[V*_{t+1}(s')]] (20.2), from t = T−1 down to 0.

Unit 2 · SL-02 · C02

LQR: the tractable special case

From the lessonSL-02 §Source anchor, §Why this shape

LQR is the tractable special case of the finite-horizon MDP: continuous states and actions S = R^d, A = R^d, linear dynamics with Gaussian noise s_{t+1} = A_t s_t + B_t a_t + w_t, w_t ∼ N(0, Σ_t), and quadratic rewards R^(t)(s_t, a_t) = −s_tᵀU_t s_t − a_tᵀW_t a_t, with U_t, W_t positive definite, so rewards are always negative (costs). The notes' example: U = W = I gives R = −||s||² − ||a||². A car that wants to stay in the lane center (small ||s||) with smooth steering (small ||a||). Linear dynamics plus quadratic costs keep the value function quadratic at every time step. That closure is the whole trick: it reduces the backward recursion to matrix algebra.

From the lessonSL-02 §Correctness checks, §Failure case

U_t and W_t must be positive definite. Check eigenvalues > 0. A zero eigenvalue means some direction is free: the "cost" is not a cost there. A reward with a positive eigenvalue in U: the "optimal" policy drives the state to infinity along that direction. The Riccati recursion still runs. The answer is garbage.

Why it matters. Closure under the Bellman update turns an infinite-dimensional problem into matrix algebra. The shape of the problem is the solution method.

Transfer. Look for the function class closed under your update. Inside it, the hard problem becomes arithmetic.

Unit 3 · SL-03 · C03

Linearization: the Taylor step

From the lessonSL-03 §Source anchor, §The Taylor step

Real dynamics are nonlinear: the inverted pendulum transition s_{t+1} = F(s_t, a_t) has cosines of the angle. LQR needs linear dynamics. The fix: linearize around a nominal point. Suppose the system spends most time near s̄_t with actions near ā_t. First-order Taylor: s_{t+1} ≈ F(s̄_t, ā_t) + ∇_s F (s_t − s̄_t) + ∇_a F (a_t − ā_t) (20.3). Rewrite as s_{t+1} ≈ As_t + Ba_t + κ with A = ∇_s F, B = ∇_a F, κ = F(s̄, ā) − As̄ − Bā. The constant κ is absorbed by adding a dimension of ones to the state, the same trick as the bias term in linear regression.

From the lessonSL-03 §Failure case

Linearizing a pendulum at the downward equilibrium and asking LQR to drive it up: the linear model is valid near down, the task lives far from down. The controller stabilizes the wrong equilibrium.

Unit 4 · SL-04 · C04

The Riccati recursion, with the corrected sign

From the lessonSL-04 §Source anchor, §The quadratic closure, §Intuition

If V*_{t+1}(s) = sᵀΦ_{t+1}s + Ψ_{t+1}, then V*_t is also quadratic. The optimal policy is linear in the state. The first-order condition gives the corrected gain: a*_t = −(B_tᵀΦ_{t+1}B_t − W_t)⁻¹ B_tᵀΦ_{t+1}A_t s_t =: L_t s_t. Source erratum: the notes print this gain without the leading minus sign. The derivation and the numeric check both require it. With Φ negative definite the printed formula points the wrong way: it pushes the state away from zero. Substituting back gives the discrete Riccati equations: Φ_t = A_tᵀ(Φ_{t+1} − Φ_{t+1}B_t(B_tᵀΦ_{t+1}B_t − W_t)⁻¹B_tᵀΦ_{t+1})A_t − U_t, and Ψ_t = −tr(Σ_t Φ_{t+1}) + Ψ_{t+1}. Φ_t is the cost-to-go matrix: how much future cost each state direction carries. The recursion propagates costs backward. L_t trades control effort (W_t) against state cost (Φ_{t+1}) through the control authority B_t.

From the lessonSL-04 §Fact 3: noise independence

Φ_t depends on neither Ψ nor Σ_t. Since L_t uses only A_t, B_t, Φ_{t+1}, the optimal policy is independent of the noise. The noise enters only Ψ_t, hence only the value, not the policy. The notes use E[wᵀΦw] = tr(ΣΦ) for w ∼ N(0, Σ).

Unit 5 · SL-05 · C05

DDP: iterate the linearization along the trajectory

From the lessonSL-05 §Source anchor, §The loop, §Why it works

Linearization (SL-03) handles staying near one state. DDP handles following a trajectory, for example a rocket flight path. Step 1: build a nominal trajectory with a naive controller approximating the desired path. Step 2: linearize the dynamics around each trajectory point, giving time-varying A_t, B_t. Expand the reward to second order around each point. Step 3: run LQR on this time-varying linear-quadratic problem to get an improved policy. Step 4: roll out the new policy on the true nonlinear dynamics F (not the linearization) to get a new nominal trajectory. Repeat from step 2 until a stopping criterion. Each iteration solves exactly the problem the linearization describes. If the new trajectory stays near the old one, the linearization stays valid and the policy improves. The notes flag the failure mode: the LQR solution can deviate far from the linearized trajectory. Reward shaping keeps the iterates close.

From the lessonSL-05 §Failure case

The naive controller in step 1 is so bad that the nominal trajectory leaves the region where any linearization is valid. DDP iterates on fiction. A feasible warm start is not optional.

Unit6

Unit 6 · SL-06 · C06

LQG: the Kalman filter plus LQR

From the lessonSL-06 §Source anchor, §The LQG setup, §The separation strategy, §The Kalman filter

Real sensors give observations: a camera image is not the full state. Linear Gaussian observations and dynamics, quadratic reward on the true state and action. Gaussian beliefs stay Gaussian. The separation strategy: (1) maintain the belief s_t | y_{1..t} ∼ N(s_{t|t}, Σ_{t|t}) with the Kalman filter. (2) use the mean s_{t|t} as the state estimate. (3) act with a_t = L_t s_{t|t}, L_t from plain LQR. Why this works: the estimate is a noisy version of the state, and LQR's policy is noise-independent (Fact 3, SL-04). Predict: s_{t+1|t} = As_{t|t}, Σ_{t+1|t} = AΣ_{t|t}Aᵀ + Σ_s. Update: K_t = Σ_{t+1|t}Cᵀ(CΣ_{t+1|t}Cᵀ + Σ_y)⁻¹, s_{t+1|t+1} = s_{t+1|t} + K_t(y_{t+1} − Cs_{t+1|t}), Σ_{t+1|t+1} = Σ_{t+1|t} − K_tCΣ_{t+1|t}. The naive marginal computation would invert t × t matrices at O(t⁴) total. The filter updates in constant time per step. The notes note the filter flew on the Apollo Lunar Module.

Unit7

Unit 7 · SL-07 · C07

REINFORCE: the gradient kills what it cannot see

From the lessonSL-07 §Source anchor, §The log-derivative trick, §Interpretation

REINFORCE is model-free: no value function, no Q function, no learned model. It needs only samples from P_sa and queries of R(s, a). The policy is randomized: π_θ(a|s). The log-derivative trick: ∇_θ E_{τ∼P_θ}[f(τ)] = E_{τ∼P_θ}[(∇_θ log P_θ(τ)) f(τ)] (21.3). Move the gradient inside the integral, use ∇P = P∇log P, and note f does not depend on θ. Expand log P_θ(τ): the gradient kills every term that does not depend on θ, and the unknown transitions vanish. What remains: ∇_θ η(θ) = E[(Σ_t ∇_θ log π_θ(a_t|s_t)) f(τ)] (21.8). Sample n trajectories, average, and take a gradient ascent step. The estimate is unbiased. ∇log P_θ(τ) is the score: the direction that increases the log-probability of the sampled trajectory. f(τ) weights it. Rewarding trajectories get their direction reinforced strongly.

Unit 8 · SL-08 · C08

Baselines: same mean, smaller variance

From the lessonSL-08 §Source anchor

The REINFORCE estimator has high variance. Take f(τ) = 1 (constant reward). Then η is constant, its gradient is zero, so E[Σ_t ∇_θ log π_θ(a_t|s_t)] = 0 (21.9). Consequence: for any B(s_t) that depends only on the state, E[∇log π(a_t|s_t) B(s_t)] = 0. So subtracting a baseline keeps the estimator unbiased: ∇η = Σ_t E[∇log π(a_t|s_t)(R_{≥t} − B(s_t))] (21.11). The mean is unchanged. The variance can drop a lot. The notes' variance example: future reward 1000 ± small, grad log π takes values z and −z. Without a baseline the estimator takes values 1001z and −998z: huge spread, same mean. With baseline 1000: z and 2z. Same expectation, far smaller variance. The near-optimal baseline is E[R_{≥t} | s_t], essentially V^{π_θ}(s_t). Its exact value does not matter for the mean, only for the variance, so a crude fit suffices.

Unit 9 · SL-09 · C09

PPO: the local surrogate

From the lessonSL-09 §Source anchor, §The local surrogate

Vanilla policy gradient is on-policy: each batch of trajectories supports exactly one gradient update. Sampling is expensive, so PPO reuses trajectories from a recent old policy for several updates. The likelihood ratio r_t(θ) = π_θ(a_t|s_t) / π_{θ_old}(a_t|s_t) (21.15) corrects the action distribution from old to new. The importance-weighted local surrogate: J_IS(θ) = Σ_t E_{s∼d_old, a∼π_old}[r_t(θ) R_{≥t}] (21.16). Read it carefully: the ratio fixes the action expectation, but the state distribution d_old and the rollouts still come from the old policy. It is a local approximation, valid near θ_old.

From the lessonSL-09 §Computed example, §Failure case

Old policy π_old(a₁) = 0.5, new policy π(a₁) = 0.6 after one update. Sampled action a₁: r = 0.6/0.5 = 1.2. With R_{≥t} = 1, the surrogate contribution is 1.2. Many updates on one batch without clipping: r_t explodes on rare actions, the surrogate claims huge gains, and the policy jumps somewhere the samples do not support.

Clipping: stop overshooting in both directions

From the lessonSL-10 §Source anchor, §The two cases

C_t(θ) = min(r_t(θ)Â_t, clip_{[1−ε, 1+ε]}(r_t(θ)) Â_t) (21.18). J_PPO(θ) = −Σ_t E[C_t(θ)] (21.19). Minimize J_PPO, i.e. maximize the clipped sum. Â_t > 0: the sampled action beat the baseline, the update wants more of it. If r_t > 1 + ε, the gain caps at (1 + ε)Â_t: pushing further is unnecessary. Â_t < 0: the action was worse than baseline, the update wants less of it. If r_t < 1 − ε, the term caps at (1 − ε)Â_t (the min picks the less negative value): making a bad action even rarer earns no further credit. This stops overshooting in both directions.

Unit11

Unit 11 · SL-11 · C11

On-policy, and PPO's controlled excursion

From the lessonSL-11 §Source anchor, §The line, §Why the distinction matters

The terms are standard. The content is the notes' own sampling statements. On-policy: the data comes from the policy being updated. Vanilla REINFORCE and the baseline variant are on-policy: one batch, one update. PPO: mostly on-policy with a controlled excursion. It reuses trajectories from θ_old for a few gradient steps. The likelihood ratio corrects the action distribution, but the state distribution stays d_old. Clipping plus the KL regularizer keep θ near θ_old so the approximation holds. After the updates, fresh trajectories are sampled and θ_old is reset. The data is never far off-policy. Off-policy data with no correction biases the gradient: the score identity needs the sampling policy to match the updated policy. PPO's ratio corrects only the action factor. State-distribution shift is uncorrected by design and bounded by keeping the excursion small.

From the lessonSL-11 §Failure case

Reusing a batch across too many epochs: θ leaves the trust region, the state distribution shifts, and the clipped surrogate optimizes a fiction. The symptom is a policy that improves on the surrogate while true returns fall.

Unit12

Unit 12 · SL-12 · C12

Stability: three devices, one shared limit

From the lessonSL-12 §Source anchor, §What each device does, §The failure they share

The notes give three stability devices: clipping (21.18), the KL regularizer (18.2), and the baseline for variance (21.11). GAE [Schulman et al., 2016] is named as the standard advantage improvement. The notes do not derive it. Clipping bounds the per-step incentive: no single sampled action can pull the policy arbitrarily far in one update. The KL penalty bounds the global drift from a reference: it prices large policy changes. The baseline bounds the gradient noise: it keeps the update direction from drowning in reward scale. All three are local. Clipping binds only per token. The KL penalty is an expectation over sampled states. The baseline only reduces variance, it does not correct bias. None of them fixes a misspecified objective: if the reward is wrong (U15 SL-03), a stable optimizer reaches the wrong answer reliably.

From the lessonSL-12 §Failure case

Tuning only the learning rate while the reward scale drifts: the effective step size changes every batch and clipping fires constantly. Normalize advantages per batch (as GRPO does in 18.7) before blaming the optimizer.

Unit 13 · Synthesis

The arc, end to end

From the lessonSL-01 to SL-12

The arc, end to end. Finite-horizon MDPs need no discount, and the optimal policy is non-stationary: the time left is part of the decision (SL-01). LQR is the tractable special case: linear dynamics plus quadratic costs keep the value function quadratic, and the whole problem becomes matrix algebra (SL-02). Nonlinear dynamics linearize around a nominal point by the Taylor step (SL-03). The Riccati recursion propagates the cost-to-go backward, with the corrected gain sign: from s₀ = 5 the closed loop goes 5 → 2 → 1, and the policy ignores the noise (SL-04). DDP iterates linearization along a trajectory, rolling out on the true dynamics (SL-05). LQG separates estimation from control: the Kalman filter tracks the belief, LQR acts on its mean (SL-06). REINFORCE differentiates only the policy: the unknown transitions vanish from the gradient (SL-07). Baselines keep the estimator unbiased while cutting variance to zero on the bandit toy (SL-08). PPO reuses old trajectories through the likelihood ratio, a local surrogate valid near θ_old (SL-09). Clipping stops overshooting in both directions (SL-10). The data stays nearly on-policy by design (SL-11). Three stability devices bound the update, and none fixes a wrong reward (SL-12).

From the lesson§Research reading, §Role bridges

Where the unit points next, in the lesson's own forward links. Read SRC-01 chapters 20 and 21 in full, Schulman et al. [2017] sections 1-5. The extension: implement DDP on a 1d nonlinear toy (s_{t+1} = sin(s_t) + a_t) and test whether iterating the nominal trajectory beats a single linearization. The falsifiable claim: three DDP iterations reach lower closed-loop cost than one LQR solve on the initial linearization, measured over 10 random starts. For the ML engineer: the Riccati sign check (Φ negative semidefinite) belongs in every LQR pipeline's unit tests. For the research engineer: PPO's clipped fraction and KL are the two dials. Log both from the first run. For the FDE: a controller that is stable in simulation but untested on the true dynamics is a demo, not a deployment. The DDP step-4 rollout rule is the acceptance gate.
