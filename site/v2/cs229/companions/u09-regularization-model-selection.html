Unit 1 · SL-01 · C01, C04, C05

Ridge: a budget on the weights, and a prior

From the lessonSL-01 §Source anchor, §Mental model

Regularized loss J_λ = J + λR, with l2 R = (1/2)||θ||². Regularization is a budget: you may fit the data, but every unit of weight norm costs λ. Ridge shrinks all weights toward zero smoothly. Nothing becomes exactly zero. The Bayesian view: a Gaussian prior says large weights are a priori unlikely. MAP trades likelihood against prior exactly as J trades against λR.

From the lessonSL-01 §Derivation

θ_MAP = argmax ∏ p(yⁱ|xⁱ, θ) p(θ). With θ ∼ N(0, τ²I): log p(θ) = −||θ||²/(2τ²) + const. So the log-MAP objective is log likelihood − ||θ||²/(2τ²). Minimizing the negative is exactly ridge with λ = 1/τ², up to the 1/2 convention. Weight decay: GD on J_λ gives θ ← θ − η∇J_λ = (1 − ηλ)θ − η∇J (notes equation 9.2). Each step first shrinks θ by (1 − ηλ), then follows the loss gradient.

Regularizing the bias term: shrinking θ₀ toward zero biases every prediction. Standard practice excludes the intercept (notes convention).

LASSO: corners create sparsity

From the lessonSL-02 §Source anchor, §Mental model

You believe only 5 of 1000 features matter. Ridge keeps all 1000 small. How do you get actual zeros? Geometry. The l1 ball is a diamond with corners on the axes. The loss contours touch it at corners, which sit on axes, which means zeros. The l2 ball is smooth. Contact happens anywhere, almost never exactly on an axis. Corners create sparsity. l0 is the honest sparsity measure but discontinuous. L1 is the continuous surrogate. Note: l1 is incompatible with the kernel trick (the optimum is not a function of inner products).

From the lessonSL-02 §Correctness checks, §Failure case

l1 is non-differentiable at 0: use subgradients or coordinate descent, not naive GD. Standardize features before LASSO: the penalty is scale-sensitive. The failure case: LASSO on two identical features picks one arbitrarily. The choice is unstable across data perturbations. Report the instability, not just the selected set.

Unit 3 · SL-03 · C01

Dropout, at the boundary

From the lessonSL-03 §Source anchor, §What the notes establish

The notes name dropout as a common deep-learning regularizer alongside data augmentation, spectral-norm regularization, and Lipschitz regularization (page 139). The notes do not develop its mechanism. Mechanism (standard, beyond the notes): during training, randomly zero a fraction p of activations each forward pass. At test time use all units with scaled weights. Intuition: the network cannot rely on any single unit, so it learns redundant representations. It approximates training an ensemble of thinned networks.

From the lessonSL-03 §Honest boundary, §Failure case

The notes list dropout without analysis. The mechanism above is standard material outside these notes. It is taught as standard background, not as a claim from SRC-01. The failure case: forgetting to scale at test time (or equivalently using inverted dropout during training). Train and test see different activation magnitudes and performance collapses.

Unit 4 · SL-04 · C06, C07, C08, C09

Model selection: the rehearsal and its audits

From the lessonSL-04 §Source anchor, §Mental model

You have 11 polynomial degrees. Training error picks degree 10 every time. What picks the right one? The validation set is a rehearsal for deployment: data the model never trained on. Holdout uses one split. K-fold CV rotates the rehearsal role across k folds and averages, using every point for both training and validation exactly once. Cost: k training runs.

From the lessonSL-04 §Procedure

The procedure: (1) split S into train and validation, or k folds. (2) train each candidate on train only. (3) score each on validation only. (4) pick the winner, optionally refit the winner on all of S. (5) report final performance on a test set that touched nothing above.

From the lessonSL-04 §Selection bias, repeated search, and leakage

The winner's validation score is optimistic because you selected for it, and every extra round of search deepens the optimism. With d models the best-of-d validation error understates the true error. The U08 union bound quantifies the gap. Fix: a final untouched test set. Leakage: any information from validation or test entering training. Preprocessing fit on all data, feature selection on all data, tuning on the test set. Fix: every data-dependent step inside the CV loop.

From the lessonSL-04 §Failure case

"Test-set tuning": iterating on the test set until the number looks good. The test set is now a validation set and you have no test set.

Unit 5 · SL-05 · C02

The optimizer as a second regularizer

From the lessonSL-05 §Source anchor, §Mental model

Two runs reach zero training error with different test errors. The loss is identical. What differed? The optimizer is a second regularizer. Among all fits that explain the training data, GD from small initialization drifts toward small-norm solutions. It never visits the wild interpolators. The algorithm's path, not just the loss, selects the model. This is why overparameterized nets can generalize (U08 SL-02): the second descent needs a benign implicit bias.

From the lessonSL-05 §What the notes establish and what they do not, §Failure case

The notes state the phenomenon and show figures of different minima (Figures 9.1-9.2). They do not prove which bias GD has. That is active research. Taught as: observed phenomenon with named instances, open theory. The failure case: assuming implicit regularization always saves you. It is data- and architecture-dependent. On small noisy data the classical U-curve still rules.

Early stopping, learning curves, and the world

From the lessonSL-06 §Early stopping

Stop training when validation error starts rising. The number of gradient steps becomes the complexity knob: few steps keep weights near their (small) initialization, many steps fit noise. Early stopping is implicit l2 regularization with the step count as λ. Cost: nearly free, you already compute validation error. Risk: a noisy validation curve stops too early. Use patience, a fixed number of non-improving epochs.

From the lessonSL-06 §Learning curves, §Model complexity, §Deployment distribution shift bridge

Learning curves plot training and validation error versus training-set size n. Signatures: both errors high and close together means bias, and more data will not help. A gap between them means variance, and more data helps. Learning curves turn the bias-variance diagnosis into a measurement. Complexity is not just parameter count: the informative measure can be a function of the parameters (l2 norm), controlled by regularization rather than architecture size.

Regularization and selection assume the validation distribution matches deployment. When it does not (new hospital, new year, new user population), the selected λ, degree, and stopping point are tuned for the wrong world. The bridge: measure on data from the deployment distribution. Re-run selection when the world changes. Monitor the gap in production.

Unit 7 · Synthesis

The arc, end to end

From the lessonSL-01 to SL-06

The arc, end to end. Explicit penalties put a budget on the weights: ridge shrinks smoothly and is MAP under a Gaussian prior with λ = 1/τ², while LASSO's diamond geometry lands on axes and selects (SL-01, SL-02). Dropout sits in the toolbox at the notes' boundary, named on page 139 without a developed mechanism (SL-03). Selection runs on rehearsal data: holdout and k-fold CV pick the winner, and the winner's score stays optimistic until a final untouched test set (SL-04). The optimizer regularizes implicitly by the path it takes among the minima (SL-05). Early stopping turns the step count into the complexity knob, learning curves turn the bias-variance diagnosis into a measurement, and every selection voids if the deployment distribution drifts (SL-06).

From the lesson§Research reading, §Role bridges

Where the unit points next, in the lesson's own forward links. The extension: compare ridge and LASSO paths on correlated features. The falsifiable claim: LASSO zeroes one of a correlated pair while ridge shares weight. For the ML engineer: ridge plus CV-selected λ is the default fix for overfitting linear models, and leakage auditing is a deployment skill. For MLOps: the train/validation/test split discipline and the preprocessing-inside-folds rule are pipeline requirements, not suggestions. For the research engineer: implicit regularization is the reason optimizer choice is a hyperparameter in deep learning.
