Unit 1 · SL-01 · C01, C02

Three error sources

From the lessonSL-01 §Source anchor, §Mental model

The degree-5 polynomial hits every training point. Why is it worse than the quadratic on test data? Three error sources. Irreducible noise σ²: the data itself is noisy, and no model predicts it. Bias²: the model family cannot represent the truth even with infinite data, like the linear fit to a quadratic. Variance: the fit changes with each dataset because it chases noise. The degree-5 wiggles differ per dataset (notes Figure 8.7).

From the lessonSL-01 §Derivation

MSE(x) = E[(y − h_S(x))²] with y = h*(x) + ξ. Claim 8.1.1: if E[A] = 0 and A is independent of B, then E[(A+B)²] = E[A²] + E[B²]. Apply twice and the three terms separate: MSE = σ² + (h*(x) − h_avg(x))² + E[(h_avg(x) − h_S(x))²] (notes page 123). Noise, bias², variance.

From the lessonSL-01 §Correctness checks, §Failure case

bias² + variance ≥ 0. MSE ≥ σ² always. More data shrinks variance, never bias: n = 15 versus n = 1500, the linear fit stays bad (notes Figure 8.3). The failure case: "more data fixes underfitting." The linear model on infinite data still misses the quadratic. Bias is a family property, not a data property.

Nugget 1 · From the lesson SL-01 §Derivation
The lesson

MSE = σ² + bias² + variance. Noise you cannot remove, error your family cannot avoid, error your data cannot pin down.

Why it matters. The decomposition names the lever. High bias: change the family. High variance: add data or regularize. Noise: accept it. Without the split, every fix is a guess.

Transfer. Decompose any error into what the world contributes, what the model class contributes, and what the sample contributes. Each term has its own remedy.

More data shrinks variance, never bias. Infinite data will not bend a line into a quadratic.

Why it matters. Data collection is expensive and bias is immune to it. The team that gathers 10x the data for an underfitting model buys a more precise wrong answer.

Unit 2 · SL-02 · C03

Double descent: the curve with two valleys

From the lessonSL-02 §Source anchor, §Mental model

The bias-variance curve says bigger models eventually overfit. Why do 1e9-parameter nets generalize? Classical regime, params below n: the U-curve holds. Interpolation threshold, params near n: the model just barely fits the training data. The fit is forced and fragile. Test error peaks. Overparameterized regime, params above n: many fits achieve zero training error. The optimizer's implicit bias (U09) picks smooth ones. Test error descends again, sometimes below the classical minimum (notes page 125).

From the lessonSL-02 §Correctness checks, §Failure case

The peak is near params = n, not at a fixed complexity: verify by varying n. With strong explicit regularization the peak flattens: the second descent needs the lightly-regularized regime. The failure case: assuming double descent always happens. It needs overparameterization plus benign implicit bias. Small noisy tabular data often keeps the classical U.

3Nugget 3 · From the lesson SL-02 §Mental model
Nugget 3 · From the lesson SL-02 §Mental model
The lesson

The worst place is the interpolation threshold, where the model just barely fits. Past it, smoothness returns.

Why it matters. The classical advice, stop before the peak, and the modern advice, push past it, differ by one regime. Knowing which regime you are in decides which advice applies.

Transfer. When a curve has two valleys, the local minimum is a trap. The global picture needs the full axis.

Finite classes: Hoeffding plus the union bound

From the lessonSL-03 §Source anchor, §Mental model

You picked the best of k models by training error. Why should its test error be close? For one fixed h, Hoeffding says the training error concentrates around the true error: bad luck is exponentially unlikely in n. For k models, the union bound says the chance that any one of them is unlucky is at most k times the single-model chance. Pay log k samples for the privilege of choosing among k. This is uniform convergence over H.

From the lessonSL-03 §Derivation

Hoeffding: P(|ε − ε̂| > γ) ≤ 2 exp(−2γ²n). Union bound over k: P(exists i with |ε(hᵢ) − ε̂(hᵢ)| > γ) ≤ 2k exp(−2γ²n) = δ. Solve: n ≥ (1/(2γ²)) log(2k/δ). Then ERM satisfies ε(ĥ) ≤ ε(h*) + 2γ (notes corollary).

From the lessonSL-03 §Concentration assumptions, §Failure case

The load-bearing assumptions: IID draws from D, the same D for train and test, and bounded loss (0/1 here, since Hoeffding needs boundedness). The failure case: applying the bound to a model picked after seeing test data. The guarantee voids. The bound covers selection by training error only.

The lesson

Choosing among k models costs log k samples, not k. The union bound prices selection logarithmically.

Why it matters. Model selection at scale is affordable because the price is logarithmic. A million candidates cost about 14 times the log factor, not a million times.

Transfer. The cost of choosing is not the number of options. It is the log of the number of options, when the comparison is fair.

Unit 4 · SL-04 · C07, C08

Infinite classes, and where the notes stop

From the lessonSL-04 §Source anchor, §The notes' argument

A computer stores each of d parameters in 64 bits, so at most 2^(64d) distinct hypotheses exist in practice. Plug k = 2^(64d) into the finite bound: log k = 64 d log 2, so n ≥ O(d/γ² · log(1/δ)). Sample complexity linear in the parameter count d (notes page 134).

From the lessonSL-04 §Honest boundary

The 64-bit argument is a hack: the bound depends on the representation, not the learning problem. The notes do not develop VC dimension. They stop here. C08 is therefore taught as: the notes establish uniform convergence for finite H and the parameter-counting extension. VC dimension is the standard refinement, not covered in these notes. The learner is told exactly where the notes stop.

From the lessonSL-04 §Why it matters anyway, §Failure case

The takeaway survives the hack: model selection among richer families needs more data, roughly linear in the effective parameter count in the classical regime. Double descent (SL-02) is where this picture breaks. The failure case: quoting the 2^(64d) bound as a serious guarantee. It is vacuous for d = 1e9, which needs n around 1e11. Treat it as intuition, not engineering.

Unit 5 · SL-05 · C10, C11, C12

Error anatomy, IID limits, uncertainty

From the lessonSL-05 §The three errors

The total gap to the Bayes-optimal splits three ways. Approximation error: H cannot represent the truth, the bias term. Estimation error: finite n, the variance term. Optimization error: the optimizer did not find the ERM. Deep learning adds the third as a first-class citizen: non-convex training may not reach the best fit in H.

From the lessonSL-05 §IID limits, §Uncertainty

Every bound in Section 3 assumes train and test come from the same D. Break it, through time drift, a new hospital, a new city, and the guarantees void. The honest response: measure on data from the deployment distribution (U01 SL-06, U09). Two uncertainties: aleatoric, noise in the data (σ²), which more data does not remove. Epistemic uncertainty is about the model, and more data shrinks it. Calibration (U04) is the operational check.

Unit 6 · Synthesis

The arc, end to end

From the lessonSL-01 to SL-05

The arc, end to end. Test error splits into noise, bias², and variance: the quadratic wins because it pays neither the linear family's bias nor the degree-5 family's variance (SL-01). Push the parameter count past n and the curve descends again: the interpolation peak is the worst place, not the biggest model (SL-02). For finite classes, Hoeffding plus the union bound prices selection at log k samples, and ERM lands within 2γ of the best in H (SL-03).

The infinite case gets the 64-bit argument: linear in d, honest about being a hack, with VC dimension named as the refinement the notes do not develop (SL-04). The remaining gap splits into approximation, estimation, and optimization error, and every guarantee voids the moment the deployment distribution drifts (SL-05).

From the lesson§Research reading, §Role bridges

Where the unit points next, in the lesson's own forward links. The optimizer's implicit bias (U09) is what picks the smooth fit past the interpolation threshold. Calibration (U04) is the operational check on uncertainty. Measure on the deployment distribution (U01 SL-06, U09). The extension: reproduce the double-descent peak on random features and show it moves with n. The falsifiable claim: the peak tracks params = n. For the ML engineer: the decomposition is the language of production debugging. For the researcher: uniform convergence is the entry to generalization theory, with the notes' boundary marked. For MLOps: every reported error needs its distribution.
