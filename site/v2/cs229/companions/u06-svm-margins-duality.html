Two margins: the score and the distance

From the lessonSL-01 §Source anchor, §Mental model

Point A is far from the boundary, point C sits next to it (notes page 62 figure). Both are classified correctly. Which prediction deserves more confidence? The functional margin is the signed score: y⁽ⁱ⁾(wᵀx⁽ⁱ⁾ + b). Positive means correct, larger means more confident. But scaling (w, b) by 2 doubles every functional margin without changing a single prediction. The geometric margin divides by ‖w‖: it is the actual Euclidean distance to the boundary, immune to scale changes.

Geometric margin sign matches correctness. Rescaling (w, b) leaves all geometric margins fixed: test in code. The failure case: maximizing the functional margin directly. The lesson

The functional margin is a score. The geometric margin is a distance. Only the distance survives rescaling.

Why it matters. Any objective built on the functional margin is gameable by scaling. The optimizer will take the free lunch instead of solving your problem.

Transfer. Before maximizing a score, check it against trivial transformations. A metric that a rescaling can inflate is not a metric.

Unit 2 · SL-02 · C03, C04

The hard-margin primal: widest gap as an optimization

From the lessonSL-02 §Source anchor, §Derivation

How do we turn "widest separating gap" into an optimization problem a solver can eat? Want: max geometric margin γ with all examples at distance ≥ γ. With ‖w‖ = 1, geometric equals functional, but ‖w‖ = 1 is non-convex. Trick: the problem is scale-invariant, so fix the functional margin to 1 by rescaling. Then γ = 1/‖w‖, and maximizing γ equals minimizing (1/2)‖w‖². The primal: min (1/2)‖w‖² subject to y⁽ⁱ⁾(wᵀx⁽ⁱ⁾ + b) ≥ 1 for all i. Convex quadratic objective, linear constraints: solvable by QP code (notes page 67).

All constraints hold with equality on at least one example per class: the support vectors. The objective (1/2)‖w‖² falls as the gap widens. The failure case: non-separable data. No (w, b) satisfies all constraints. The primal is infeasible. SL-05 repairs this with slack.

Unit 3 · SL-03 · C05, C06

Duality: price the constraints

From the lessonSL-03 §Source anchor, §Mental model

The primal is a QP in w. Why rewrite it in terms of one variable per example? Constraints get prices: αᵢ ≥ 0 per example. The Lagrangian: L = (1/2)‖w‖² − Σᵢ αᵢ [y⁽ⁱ⁾(wᵀx⁽ⁱ⁾ + b) − 1]. Minimizing over w, b gives w = Σᵢ αᵢ y⁽ⁱ⁾ x⁽ⁱ⁾ and Σᵢ αᵢ y⁽ⁱ⁾ = 0. Plug back: the dual depends only on inner products ⟨x⁽ⁱ⁾, x⁽ʲ⁾⟩.

From the lessonSL-03 §Mental model

W(α) = Σᵢ αᵢ − (1/2) Σᵢⱼ y⁽ⁱ⁾y⁽ʲ⁾ αᵢαⱼ ⟨x⁽ⁱ⁾, x⁽ʲ⁾⟩, subject to αᵢ ≥ 0 and Σᵢ αᵢ y⁽ⁱ⁾ = 0. KKT conditions (notes 6.3 to 6.7): stationarity, primal feasibility, dual feasibility, complementary slackness: αᵢ [y⁽ⁱ⁾(wᵀx⁽ⁱ⁾ + b) − 1] = 0. Two wins: the data enters only through inner products, so kernels slot in (SL-04). The solution is sparse: most αᵢ = 0.

From the lessonSL-03 §Correctness checks, §Failure case

Complementary slackness: αᵢ > 0 implies the constraint is tight. Σᵢ αᵢ y⁽ⁱ⁾ = 0 holds at the solution. Dual value equals primal value: strong duality for this convex QP. The failure case: forgetting the Σᵢ αᵢ y⁽ⁱ⁾ = 0 constraint. Coordinate ascent on one α at a time cannot move (notes page 77). SMO updates pairs for exactly this reason.

The dual sees the data only through inner products. That single fact is the doorway kernels walk through.

Transfer. When you reformulate a problem, watch what the new form depends on. The dependencies are the extension points.

Unit 4 · SL-04 · C07, C11

Support vectors: the margin points decide

From the lessonSL-04 §Source anchor, §Mental model

αᵢ > 0 marks a support vector: an example sitting exactly on the margin, or violating it in the soft case. All other examples have αᵢ = 0 and do not affect w or predictions. The classifier is a weighted vote of the support vectors: wᵀx + b = Σᵢ αᵢ y⁽ⁱ⁾ ⟨x⁽ⁱ⁾, x⟩ + b. Replace the inner product with K: the SVM learns in infinite-dimensional feature spaces (notes page 73).

From the lessonSL-04 §Correctness checks, §Failure case

Non-support examples can be deleted without changing (w, b). Test: refit without them. b* from (6.13): the average of the margin boundaries. The failure case is a naming trap: support vectors are not the misclassified points. They are the margin points. A separable dataset has zero errors and still has support vectors.

3Nugget 3 · From the lesson SL-04 §Failure case
Nugget 3 · From the lesson SL-04 §Failure case
The lesson

Support vectors are the margin points, not the mistakes. Deleting every non-support example changes nothing.

Why it matters. Sparsity is a fact about the solution, not a heuristic. In production the kernel SVM stores only support vectors, and the storage budget follows from the theory.

Transfer. Find which inputs actually decide the output. Everything else is commentary.

Unit 5 · SL-05 · C08, C09

Soft margins: violate at a price

From the lessonSL-05 §Source anchor, §Mental model

One outlier drags the boundary across the room (notes page 74 figure). How do we let examples violate the margin at a price? Slack ξᵢ measures the margin violation in functional units. Pay C·ξᵢ per unit. Large C: violations are expensive, the boundary bends to outliers, hard-margin behavior. Small C: violations are cheap, the margin stays wide and some examples fall inside or across.

From the lessonSL-05 §The dual change

Primal: min (1/2)‖w‖² + C Σᵢ ξᵢ subject to y⁽ⁱ⁾(wᵀx⁽ⁱ⁾ + b) ≥ 1 − ξᵢ, ξᵢ ≥ 0. The dual change is only the box: 0 ≤ αᵢ ≤ C. Everything else is the same W(α). This is the surprising result the notes highlight (page 74).

Each example falls in exactly one KKT case. Verify on the solution. C → infinity recovers hard margin on separable data: test it. The failure case: C picked by test accuracy. The test set is spent. Pick C by cross-validation (U09).

The soft-margin dual changes only the box: 0 ≤ αᵢ ≤ C. One parameter prices every violation.

Why it matters. Small changes in the right place beat redesigns. The entire robustness story of the SVM lives in one bound on α.

Transfer. When a method needs a soft version, look for the smallest change that prices the violation. C is the whole story here.

Unit 6 · SL-06 · C10

SMO: update pairs, not singles

From the lessonSL-06 §Source anchor, §Mental model

The dual has n variables and one linear constraint. How do we maximize it without a generic QP solver? Coordinate ascent: hold all but one α fixed, optimize that one. Blocked: the Σᵢ αᵢ y⁽ⁱ⁾ = 0 constraint pins a single α given the rest. So update two at a time. The constraint becomes a line, the box [0, C]² clips the segment to [L, H], and the 1-D quadratic has a closed-form max. Clip it, update both, repeat. KKT violations pick the next pair.

From the lessonSL-06 §Complexity, §Failure case

Each pair update is O(1) given cached errors. A full pass is O(n²) kernel evals naive, O(n) with caching. SMO beats generic QP by exploiting the structure. The failure case: updating one α at a time. Zero progress, forever (notes page 77). The pair update is not an optimization. It is a feasibility requirement.

One α at a time cannot move. The pair update is not an optimization trick. It is a feasibility requirement.

Unit 7 · SL-07 · C12

Separability: when the story applies

From the lessonSL-07 §Mental model, §Diagnostic

Hard margin assumes separability. Soft margin assumes a good trade-off exists at some C. Kernels assume the feature space separates the data: more likely in high dimensions, not guaranteed. None of these assume the test data follows the same geometry. That is the IID assumption again (U01).

Diagnostic: fit hard-margin. If infeasible, the data is not separable in the current features. Options: add slack (C), change the kernel, add features. The failure case: a kernel that separates training perfectly with tiny σ. Separability achieved, generalization destroyed. Separability is necessary, not sufficient.

Unit8

Unit 8 · Synthesis

The arc, end to end

From the lessonSL-01 to SL-07

The arc, end to end. Confidence needs a distance, not a score: the geometric margin survives rescaling while the functional margin inflates (SL-01). Fix the functional margin to 1 and the widest gap becomes a convex QP: min (1/2)‖w‖² with linear constraints (SL-02). Price each constraint with αᵢ, minimize over w, and the dual depends only on inner products, with most αᵢ at zero (SL-03).

Only the margin points matter: support vectors carry the classifier, and kernels replace the inner product to learn in infinite-dimensional spaces (SL-04). Slack prices violations at C per unit, and the dual changes only by the box 0 ≤ αᵢ ≤ C (SL-05). The linear equality forces pair updates: SMO reoptimizes two alphas analytically, clipped to [L, H], with KKT violations choosing the pairs (SL-06). And the whole story assumes separability somewhere: hard margin in input space, soft margin at some C, or the kernel's feature space (SL-07).

From the lesson§Research reading, §Nearest alternatives

Where the unit points next, in the lesson's own forward links. The perceptron from U03 draws any separator with no margin preference. The primal picks the widest. Kernel logistic regression: dense α, probabilistic output, no sparsity. Hinge loss with L2 in the primal, solved with SGD: the same model, a different solver, no α sparsity certificate. Read SRC-01 chapter 6 in full, plus Platt's SMO paper for the heuristics. The extension: implement SMO-lite and plot W(α) and KKT violations per pass. The falsifiable claim: violations fall geometrically and the support set stabilizes before full convergence.
